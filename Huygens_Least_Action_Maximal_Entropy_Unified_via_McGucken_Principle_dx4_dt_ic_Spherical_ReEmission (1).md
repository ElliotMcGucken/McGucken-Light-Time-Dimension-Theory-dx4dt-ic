### 1.2 The claim, stated precisely up front

Two distinct propositions are routinely conflated under "the second law follows from spreading." We separate them and prove them separately, because the distinction is the entire methodological content of the paper.

> **(A) Monotone arrow.** The angular-mode count on the expanding McGucken Sphere increases strictly with parameter time; hence a coarse-grained entropy defined from it increases strictly. This is an *arrow*, proved in Theorem 3. It is **not** the second law of thermodynamics.

## 3. The kinematic spreading entropy (G1)

### 3.1 Angular-mode count

**Definition 3 (Angular-mode count).** Given the wavefront’s wavelength (mode-spacing) λ, the number of independent transverse modes resolvable on S²(p; t) is

  N(t) := A(t) / λ² = 4π c²(t − t₀)² / λ².

This is dimensionless: (length²)/(length²). **No Newton constant appears.** It counts how many λ-sized independent patches tile the wavefront — the angular resolution of the sphere at scale λ.

**Definition 4 (Coarse-grained spreading entropy).** S(t) := k_B ln N(t) = k_B ln [4π c²(t − t₀)² / λ²].

**Remark 1 (Why ln N and not N).** Two natural entropies attach to the wavefront. The *configurational* count of distinguishable single-excitation locations is N(t) itself, giving a Boltzmann entropy S_B = k_B ln N(t) (log of the number of accessible patches for one quantum of excitation). The *extensive* count, if each of the N patches independently carries one bit, gives S_ext = k_B N(t) ln 2. The two differ by whether excitations are sparse (one quantum spreading — use ln N) or dense (one bit per patch — use N). For the spreading of a localized initial disturbance, the sparse reading S_B = k_B ln N is correct; the extensive reading is the black-hole/holographic reading and we do not use it, since it is exactly the reading that, with λ = ℓ_P, reproduces the area law and drags in G. **Choosing the sparse reading is what keeps the construction G-free.**

**Remark 1′ (The spreading entropy is a Shannon entropy of position).** The quantity S(t) = k_B ln N(t) is, equivalently and exactly, the Shannon entropy of the photon's position distribution on the wavefront. A quantum spread isotropically over N(t) distinguishable angular cells of equal probability p_i = 1/N(t) has Shannon entropy H = −Σ_i p_i log₂ p_i = log₂ N(t) bits; multiplying by k_B ln 2 to convert bits to thermodynamic units gives S = k_B ln N(t), which is S(t). The Boltzmann reading (log of the count of accessible microstates) and the Shannon reading (uncertainty of the position distribution) coincide here because the isotropic distribution is uniform over the N(t) cells — and by Theorem 6 the realized distribution *is* the uniform one, so the coincidence is exact, not approximate. The arrow of Theorem 3 is therefore the statement that the Shannon information required to locate the photon on the wavefront grows as the sphere expands: at emission the photon is localized and H is low; as the McGucken Sphere expands, the position probability dilutes over N(t) = A(t)/λ² cells and H rises as log₂(A(t)/λ²). The spreading entropy, the Boltzmann entropy, and the Shannon entropy of position are one quantity, read in three vocabularies.

### 3.2 The arrow

**Theorem 3 (Monotone spreading arrow, G1).** *For t > t₀, the spreading entropy S(t) = k_B ln N(t) is strictly increasing, with*

  dS/dt = k_B · (dN/dt)/N(t) = k_B · 2/(t − t₀) > 0.

*Hence the spreading process defines a strictly monotone arrow of time, using only c and λ, with no horizon, no mass, and no Newton constant.*

*Proof.* N(t) = 4π c²(t−t₀)²/λ². Then dN/dt = 8π c²(t−t₀)/λ², and

  dS/dt = k_B (dN/dt)/N = k_B · [8π c²(t−t₀)/λ²] / [4π c²(t−t₀)²/λ²] = k_B · 2/(t−t₀).

For t > t₀ this is strictly positive. The λ-dependence cancels in the logarithmic derivative, so the *rate* of entropy increase is independent of the mode-spacing — a clean signature that the arrow is kinematic, set by the expansion alone. ∎

**Corollary 3.1 (Logarithmic-in-radius growth).** Since R(t) = c(t−t₀), S(t) = 2k_B ln R(t) + const, so S grows logarithmically in wavefront radius. Equivalently S = k_B ln A + const: the spreading entropy is the log of the area, **not** the area. The area-law (S ∝ A) is the *dense/extensive* reading; the spreading process gives the *sparse/logarithmic* reading. This is the precise formal point at which the present construction diverges from the Bekenstein–Hawking lineage, and it is exactly the divergence that removes G.

**Remark 2 (Grade).** Theorem 3 is **G1**: it uses the wavefront’s rate c and wavelength λ only. It does not use G, ℏ, mass, or temperature. It is an arrow, not yet the second law (Section 5).

## 4. Does the bound survive area growth? (the injectivity question, settled in the horizon-free setting)

In the holographic setting one wants N_bulk(t+dt) ≤ N_modes(t), and the danger is that the area *grows*, so the later surface has more cells than the earlier one — the encoding must run inner→outer against an increasing target. Here we confront the same question in the clean setting and settle it, because it is the one genuinely load-bearing inequality.

**Proposition 4 (No super-area mode inflation).** *Let the bulk shell between R(t) and R(t+dt) be sourced by the secondary wavelets of the N(t) surface patches at time t, per Definition 2. Then the number of independent modes deliverable into the shell does not exceed N(t):*

  N_shell(t→t+dt) ≤ N(t).

*Proof.* By Lemma 2 the forward-envelope map E_{t→t+dt} is a surjection of S²(p;t) onto S²(p;t+dt): every point of the later front is the image of at least one source point on the earlier front (it lies on the envelope of their wavelets). A surjection cannot increase the cardinality of an independent generating set: each independent mode delivered to the shell is, by Definition 2, sourced by at least one of the N(t) surface patches, and independence downstream requires independence upstream (two modes identical at the source cannot become independent under a deterministic linear propagation map). Hence the count of independent delivered modes is at most the count of independent sources, N(t). The area at t+dt is larger, but the *additional* angular resolution of the larger sphere is not sourced — it is interpolated by the envelope, hence not independent. Independence is inherited from the source set, whose size is N(t), not from the geometric size of the target. ∎

**Remark 3 (Why this works here but is only sketched in the horizon version).** The horizon argument invoked "a standard injectivity argument from coding theory," which asserts the conclusion. Here the conclusion is *earned* from Lemma 2: the propagation map is an explicit surjection of fronts, and the inheritance of independence is a property of deterministic linear maps, not an appeal to coding. The area growth is neutralized precisely because the new angular resolution of the larger sphere is interpolated, not sourced. This is the horizon-free analogue of the Bekenstein bound, and it holds without G because no Planck-cell identification was used — λ remains free.

## 5. From arrow to second law: the maximization statement (Jaynes)

Theorem 3 gives (A), the arrow. We now prove (B), that the realized distribution is the maximum-entropy one. This is the step that the spreading picture does **not** give for free, and conflating it with the arrow is the central error we are avoiding.

### 5.1 The variational problem

Let ρ(**n**, t) be the distribution over forward directions **n** ∈ S² of the re-emitted amplitude's probability weight at time t, normalized ∫_{S²} ρ dΩ = 1. The Gibbs/Shannon entropy of this angular distribution is

  𝓢[ρ] = −k_B ∫_{S²} ρ(**n**) ln ρ(**n**) dΩ.

**Constraints.** (C1) normalization ∫ ρ dΩ = 1. (C2) isotropy of the *generator*: the dynamics (Definition 2) impose no preferred direction, i.e. no nontrivial directional moment is fixed by the process — the only datum is normalization. (If a drift or anisotropy were imposed, it would enter as a moment constraint ∫ **n** ρ dΩ = **m**.)

**Theorem 6 (Isotropy is the maximum-entropy distribution, G1).** *Under constraints (C1)–(C2), the unique distribution maximizing 𝓢[ρ] is the uniform (isotropic) distribution ρ\*(**n**) = 1/(4π). Therefore the isotropic re-emission realized by the Huygens process (Definition 2) is the maximum-entropy distribution consistent with the rate constraint; the spreading process does not merely increase entropy, it occupies the entropy-maximizing angular distribution at each instant.*

*Proof.* Maximize 𝓢[ρ] subject to (C1) by Lagrange multipliers (Jaynes' MaxEnt construction). Form

  𝓛[ρ] = −k_B ∫ ρ ln ρ dΩ − α(∫ ρ dΩ − 1).

Functional stationarity δ𝓛/δρ = 0 gives −k_B(ln ρ + 1) − α = 0, hence ln ρ = −(1 + α/k_B) = const, so ρ* is constant on S². Normalization (C1) fixes the constant: ρ* = 1/(4π). The entropy functional is strictly concave in ρ (since −ρ ln ρ has second derivative −1/ρ < 0), so the stationary point is the unique global maximum. With no directional moment fixed (C2), no exponential tilting factor e^{β·n} appears, and the maximizer remains uniform. ∎

**Remark 4 (This is the genuine second-law content).** Theorem 6 is the statement that earns the name "second law" for the spreading process: at each instant the realized angular distribution is the one of maximum entropy given what the dynamics constrain. Combined with Theorem 3 (the accessible set N(t) grows), we have both ingredients of a real second law — a growing accessible space *and* maximal occupation within it. Neither alone suffices; the black-hole framing typically supplies only the first and asserts the second.

**Remark 5 (The Hadamard constraint — stated honestly).** The clean "sharp wavefront with no interior tail" picture used in Definition 2 and Lemma 2 is valid as stated only in **odd spatial dimension** (n = 3, 5, …). This is Hadamard's descent theorem: the wave equation satisfies the strong (sharp, tail-free) Huygens principle only in odd spatial dimensions; in even spatial dimension (n = 2, 4, …) an impulsive source leaves a persistent *wake tail* inside the light cone, so the wavefront is not sharp and the "forward envelope" picture acquires an interior contribution. Two consequences must be recorded rather than hidden:

1. The construction's cleanest form is a fact about 3-dimensional space specifically — which is the physical case, and a point in the framework's favor (3-space is selected as the dimension in which Huygens sourcing is sharp), but it is a dimensional restriction, not a universal theorem.
2. In even dimensions, the mode count N(t) = A(t)/λ² still bounds the *sharp-front* modes, but the wake tail carries additional interior degrees of freedom not captured by the surface count; Proposition 4 then bounds only the front contribution. We do not claim the construction extends unchanged to even dimensions. The odd-dimension restriction is a genuine boundary of the result.

## 6. The coherent/incoherent split: least action and entropy from one process

We now show that the *same* secondary-wavelet sourcing produces, by its two projections, the least-action content (Fermat/stationary phase) and the entropy content (decoherent spreading).

### 6.1 Stationary phase is the forward envelope

Give each secondary wavelet a phase φ = k·(path length). The amplitude at a forward point P is the superposition over source points q of e^{iφ(q,P)}. By the method of stationary phase, the dominant contribution as k→∞ comes from source points where δφ/δq = 0 — the stationary-phase points — and these are precisely the points whose wavelets arrive *in phase*, i.e. the points on the forward envelope. Off-envelope contributions oscillate and cancel.

**Theorem 8 (Least action and entropy as coherent/incoherent projections, G1).** *The secondary-wavelet superposition decomposes into:*

*(i) a **coherent** part supported on the stationary-phase locus — the forward envelope — whose selection condition δφ = 0 is Fermat's principle of stationary optical path, equivalently the least-action condition for the propagation; and*

*(ii) an **incoherent** part, the off-envelope directions, whose mutual phase cancellation is the loss of which-path coherence and whose angular spreading carries the entropy of Theorem 3 and Theorem 6.*

*The two are projections of one isotropic re-emission: the coherent projection yields least action, the incoherent projection yields the second law.*

*Proof.* (i) The stationary-phase condition δφ(q,P)/δq = 0 selects, among all source points q on S²(p;t), those for which the path q→P is an extremum of optical length. For propagation at uniform rate c this extremum is the straight radial continuation, and the locus of P reached in phase from the whole sphere is the envelope S²(p;t+dt) (Definition 2). The extremal-path condition is Fermat's principle; under the optical-mechanical analogy (Maupertuis–Hamilton), the stationary optical path is the stationary action. Hence the coherent part is governed by least action.

(ii) The complementary off-stationary directions receive contributions e^{iφ} with δφ ≠ 0; by stationary phase these sum to a subdominant, rapidly oscillating remainder. Their relative phases are uncorrelated at scale λ (one mode-spacing being the coherence patch), so the information about which source produced a given off-envelope amplitude is not recoverable at resolution λ — this is decoherence of the path label. The angular distribution of this incoherent weight is, by the isotropy of the generator (Definition 2) and Theorem 6, the uniform maximum-entropy distribution, whose accessible-patch count grows as N(t) (Theorem 3). Hence the incoherent projection carries exactly the spreading entropy. ∎

**Remark 6 (Interpretation).** This makes precise the sense in which least action and the second law are two faces of one spreading process: coherence picks the extremal path (action), decoherence spreads the residue isotropically (entropy). The McGucken Principle supplies the single underlying re-emission; the projection chosen — in-phase envelope vs. out-of-phase spread — determines which classical principle is read off. No horizon and no G appear in either projection.

### 6.2 The unification, stated precisely

We now state the unification as a formal partition of a single amplitude, with the two classical principles as its two projections, controlled by one parameter. We then state, as explicit scope conditions, the three hypotheses on which the result rests.

**Definition 6.2.1 (The wavefront amplitude and its stationary partition).** Let P be a forward field point and let {q_i} index the secondary sources on the McGucken Sphere S²(p;t), each contributing a wavelet of unit modulus and phase φ_i = S_i/ℏ, where S_i is the action along the path q_i → P. The wavefront amplitude is

  𝒜(P) = Σ_i exp(iS_i/ℏ).

Fix a coherence tolerance set by the mode-spacing λ. Define the **stationary neighborhood** Σ_∥ = { q_i : |δS_i| ≲ ℏ across the λ-patch of q_i } and its complement, the **off-stationary set** Σ_⊥ = { q_i : |δS_i| ≫ ℏ }. The corresponding projectors Π_∥, Π_⊥ = 1 − Π_∥ partition the amplitude:

  𝒜(P) = Π_∥𝒜(P) + Π_⊥𝒜(P) ≕ 𝒜_∥(P) + 𝒜_⊥(P).

This partition is canonical given (𝒜, λ): Σ_∥ is determined by the stationarity condition δS = 0 to within the λ-patch, and Σ_⊥ is its complement. No further choice enters.

**Theorem 6.2.2 (The two projections are least action and maximum entropy).** *Under Hypotheses (H1)–(H3) below, the partition of Definition 6.2.1 satisfies:*

*(i) (Coherent projection → least action.) In the limit ℏ → 0, 𝒜_∥(P) is dominated by the stationary-action path q* with δS|_{q*} = 0, and the realized trajectory is the classical least-action (Fermat) path. This is the Dirac–Feynman stationary-phase result.*

*(ii) (Incoherent projection → maximum entropy.) After coarse-graining 𝒜_⊥ at scale λ (Hypothesis H1), the surviving angular weight is a probability distribution over the N(t) = A(t)/λ² cells of S²(p;t). Subject to the only constraints carried by the generator — normalization and isotropy (Hypothesis H2) — this distribution is, by Jaynes' theorem (Theorem 6), the uniform maximum-entropy distribution, with Shannon/Boltzmann entropy S(t) = k_B ln N(t) (Remark 1′).*

*(iii) (Single-parameter control.) Both projections are governed by the single ratio S/ℏ through the one phase φ = S/ℏ. The stationary neighborhood is the region δS ≲ ℏ; the off-stationary set is δS ≫ ℏ. Coherent and incoherent are therefore not two processes but two phase-regimes of the one superposition 𝒜(P), separated by the complementary projectors Π_∥, Π_⊥.*

*Proof.* (i) On Σ_∥ the phases S_i/ℏ agree to within unity across each λ-patch, so the wavelets add in phase. By the stationary-phase/steepest-descent theorem, as ℏ → 0 the integral Σ_{Σ_∥} exp(iS_i/ℏ) is dominated by an O(√ℏ) neighborhood of the stationary point q* where δS = 0, with subleading terms suppressed by powers of ℏ. The stationary condition δS = 0 is, under the Maupertuis–Hamilton optico-mechanical analogy (Section 9.1), the least-action condition; for uniform-rate propagation it is the Fermat stationary-path condition selecting the forward envelope (Theorem 8(i)). This is exactly Dirac (1933)–Feynman (1948).

No entropy decreases: the surviving sliver's narrower support is purchased by the absorbed majority's irreversible thermalization. **Entropy: increasing, located in the barrier.**

**Stage 3 — The two openings as secondary sources (re-expansion).** Each slit point is an event from which dx₄/dt = ic expands a fresh McGucken Sphere (Definition 2, the iterated-sphere property). For a transmitted photon the amplitude is now a coherent two-lobe superposition |ψ⟩ = (|A⟩ + |B⟩)/√2, still pure, fine-grained entropy zero. **Stage 4b — Which-path detector present: the incoherent projection.** A detector at a slit becomes correlated with the path: the joint state is (|A⟩|d_A⟩ + |B⟩|d_B⟩)/√2, still globally pure (category T). But the photon's reduced state, tracing out the detector, is mixed:

*Proof.* Stage 1 and Stage 3 increase the positional entropy strictly by Proposition 6.3.1. ## 7. What is observed, what is conserved, and the status of the principle

This section fixes the epistemic categories the rest of the paper uses, because the spreading entropy and its reversibility force the distinction explicitly. We adopt the operational stance that *the content of physics is the observed* — and then state precisely where each claim of this paper sits relative to observation.

### 7.1 Three categories

**(O) Observed.** Entropy increases; information is lost. This is what every measurement reports. Over any realized propagation, the coarse-grained spreading entropy of Theorem 3 rises; an image projected and then released disperses beyond recovery through finite-aperture loss, decoherence into the environment, and the fall of detected photons per resolution element below the shot-noise floor. Operationally, information *is* lost, and the second law *is* observed. The construction's arrow (Theorem 3) and the maximization (Theorem 6) live here: they describe observed coarse-grained behavior.

**(T) Unobserved theorem.** Under unitary (interaction-free) evolution, the global fine-grained state is preserved: the von Neumann entropy of the total state is constant, and the initial configuration is mathematically determined by the final one. This is a *theorem* — it follows from U†U = 1 — and it is **unobservable in principle**, because confirming it would require capturing the entire fine-grained wavefront including the correlations that have decohered into the environment, which no observer embedded in the system can collect. Information conservation is therefore not an observation and must not be presented as one. It is an unobserved theorem.

**(P) Generative principle.** dx₄/dt = ic is the generative principle of the framework: the fourth dimension expands at the velocity of light from every spacetime event. It is empirical physics in exactly the sense the Einstein field equations and Newton's laws are empirical physics — a generative law whose consequences are observed and measured (proper time, the conservation laws, the spreading entropy of this paper), and which is confirmed through those consequences. It stands in the same column as general relativity: a foundational law, stated as such, carried by what it generates.

To make the company explicit: the following are all unobserved — none is a direct meter reading, each is confirmed (where it is confirmed) only through its observed consequences. They are nonetheless physics, by the consequence criterion of Section 7.2.

*Foundational laws and action principles.* Newton's laws of motion and his law of universal gravitation; Maxwell's equations; the Einstein field equations G_μν + Λg_μν = (8πG/c⁴)T_μν; the principle of least action and the Lagrangian/Hamiltonian formulations; Fermat's principle of least time; Hamilton's principle; the Schrödinger equation; the Dirac equation; the Klein–Gordon and Yang–Mills field equations; the geodesic equation; dx₄/dt = ic itself.

*Symmetry principles and the theorems built on them.* Lorentz and Poincaré invariance; general covariance / diffeomorphism invariance; gauge invariance (U(1), SU(2), SU(3)); CPT symmetry; Noether's theorem; the spin-statistics theorem; the PCT theorem; Wigner's classification; Goldstone's theorem; Lovelock's theorem. A symmetry is never observed; what is observed is the conserved quantity or selection rule it forces.

*Structural posits and invariants.* The unitarity of quantum evolution (U†U = 1); the canonical commutation relation [q̂, p̂] = iℏ; the wavefunction and the state vector; the quantum fields themselves; the metric tensor and spacetime curvature; the gauge potential A_μ (only field strengths and phases register); the equivalence principle; conservation of energy, momentum, charge, and baryon number *as laws* (the conserved quantities are measured; the conservation principle is the generative statement).

*Theorems of general relativity and quantum theory.* The Hawking–Penrose singularity theorems; Birkhoff's theorem; the positive-energy theorem; the black-hole area theorem; Bell's theorem; the no-cloning and no-signalling theorems; Gleason's theorem; the Reeh–Schlieder theorem. None is meter-read; each constrains what observation can find.

*Constants and structures fixed by, but not identical to, measurement.* The speed of light c, Planck's constant ℏ, and Newton's constant G are *measured*, but the principles in which they are embedded (that c is the invariant rate, that ℏ is the action quantum, that G sets the curvature coupling) are generative posits, not observations.

Every entry above is unobserved in precisely the sense dx₄/dt = ic is unobserved, and physics in precisely the sense dx₄/dt = ic is physics: generators of the observed, judged by their consequences. The list is, in effect, the whole load-bearing structure of theoretical physics — which is the point. To demand that a generative principle be directly observed before counting as physics would exile this entire catalogue, Newton and Einstein included. The McGucken Principle asks for no exemption the rest of physics does not already enjoy.

### 7.2 The criterion that keeps (T) and (P) inside physics

If "physics is the observed" were read as "only the observed is physics," categories (T) and (P) — and with them unitarity, the conservation laws *as theorems*, and the McGucken Principle itself — would fall outside physics. We do not read it that way, and the reason is a consequence criterion, not a loosening of operationalism:

> An unobserved claim is physical when it is *observationally consequential* — when it constrains, generates, or forbids observations — and metaphysical only when it is inconsequential, changing no prediction and forbidding nothing.

By this criterion: the unobserved theorem (T) is physics because it **forbids** non-unitary evolution and thereby generates real tension whenever information appears to vanish — it does work even though it is never seen. The generative principle (P) is physics because it **generates** the observed (Huygens propagation, the conservation laws, the spreading entropy of this paper) and forbids configurations inconsistent with spherical x₄-expansion. Neither is idle; both have observable consequences without being observable themselves. The dividing line is consequence, not visibility — and it is the same line that separates a conserved theoretical quantity from a vacuous "in-principle" assertion that constrains nothing.

### 7.3 The reversibility statement, in these categories (Loschmidt)

**Proposition 7 (Spreading entropy is coarse-grained; its irreversibility is boundary-conditioned).** *For interaction-free (unitary) propagation of the wavefront:*

*(i) the fine-grained von Neumann entropy of the total state is constant in time (category T);*

*(ii) the coarse-grained spreading entropy S(t) = k_B ln N(t) of Theorem 3 increases strictly (category O);*

*(iii) the time-reversed solution — a converging wavefront with decreasing coarse-grained entropy — is an exact and equally valid solution of the dynamics; its non-occurrence is a fact about realized boundary conditions (localized sources occur; conspiratorial inbound-coordinated spheres do not), not about the dynamics.*

*Proof.* (i) Unitary evolution conserves von Neumann entropy: S(ρ) = S(UρU†) since U is invertible and entropy is unitarily invariant. (ii) Theorem 3. (iii) The wave dynamics are time-reversal symmetric (the operator V = ∂_t + ic∂_{x₄} together with the second-order wave operator admits t → −t with the conjugate solution); hence for every diverging solution the time-reverse is a solution. That diverging solutions are realized and converging ones are not follows from the statistics of accessible initial data — a single localized emission event specifies the diverging branch, whereas the converging branch requires phase coordination across the entire wavefront to sub-λ precision, a measure-zero condition in the space of physically prepared states (the radiation-arrow / Wheeler–Feynman observation). ∎

**Remark 7 (What this resolves).** The apparent paradox — "free spreading is reversible, so how is its entropy increase irreversible?" — is dissolved by the category split. The increase is in (O), coarse-grained and observed; the reversibility is in (T), fine-grained and theorem-level; the asymmetry that makes (O) point forward is neither dynamical nor coarse-graining-artifact alone but the boundary-condition asymmetry of (iii). The spreading entropy is exactly as irreversible as the second law generally is — i.e. statistically, by initial conditions plus coarse-graining — and no more. The paper does **not** claim free-streaming spreading is intrinsically (fine-grained) irreversible; it is not.

### 7.4 Observer-relativity of the entropy

**Remark 8 (Whose entropy S(t) is).** The spreading entropy S(t) is the *receiver's* coarse-grained entropy: it measures the uncertainty in the wavefront description available to an observer resolving at scale λ and not tracking fine position–momentum correlations. To a hypothetical preparer with the complete fine-grained state, the entropy is the constant of (T). The two are not in conflict; they are entropies of two different descriptions of one state. The construction's S(t) is, throughout, the operational (observed, category-O) quantity — which is the appropriate one, since the paper's claims are about what propagation does to accessible information, not to the inaccessible global state.

### 7.5 Scope caveat on (T)

The conservation theorem (T) holds where unitarity holds. For the interaction-free wavefront treated here, it holds cleanly. We explicitly do **not** invoke the black-hole information-paradox resolution (Page curve, islands), which is itself unconfirmed and reintroduces the G-dependent horizon machinery this paper was constructed to avoid. The unobserved theorem is claimed for the unitary free-streaming regime only.

## 8. Physics as the empirical, and the standing of generative principles

### 8.1 The thesis

The discipline of physics is grounded in observation: a theory earns its standing by being judged against experiment, and the non-empirical — that which neither is observed nor generates or constrains anything observed — is philosophy rather than physics. This paper adopts that standard. The clarifying point, established in Section 7 and made explicit here, is that a *generative principle* is not exiled to philosophy by the fact that it is not itself directly observed. Newton's law of gravitation, Maxwell's equations, the Einstein field equations, and dx₄/dt = ic are none of them "observed" as direct meter readings; each is a generative statement confirmed *through* its observed consequences. They are empirical physics in exactly the sense the great experimental tradition demands: they live or die by experiment, and they have lived.

Accordingly, dx₄/dt = ic stands in the same column as general relativity — both are empirical physics, confirmed through their observed consequences (for GR: perihelion precession, light deflection, gravitational redshift, Shapiro delay, frame-dragging, gravitational waves; for the McGucken Principle: the dual-channel descent of the confirmed empirical content of GR and QM, per [GRQM] and the verification paper). Neither is "observed" as an axiom; both are empirically grounded as generators of the observed. This is the consequence-confirmation structure, and it is precisely the structure the following testimony of the founders describes.

### 8.2 The empirical standard in the words of its founders

The compendium below is presented as the historical articulation of the standard adopted in Section 8.1. Each entry is given in paraphrase with source; quotations are held to brief, exact fragments where the precise wording carries weight. The throughline is uniform: every one of these describes physics as *judged by* observation and experiment — the generative theory proposed, its consequences computed, the comparison with measurement made decisive. None asserts that a generative principle must itself be observed; all assume the consequence-confirmation structure.

**On experiment as the arbiter.**

The same lecture holds the complementary half: the concepts are free creations of the mind, not extracted from experience by logic, and only afterward judged by it. This is the consequence-confirmation structure stated by Einstein himself.
- *Bohr* (reported, Petersen): physics concerns not how nature is, but what we can say about nature.

**On measurement as the content of knowledge.**
- *Kelvin* ("Electrical Units of Measurement," 1883): until one can measure and express in numbers, one's knowledge is of a meager and unsatisfactory kind.
- *Planck*: an experiment is a question science puts to nature; a measurement is the recording of nature's answer.
- *Heisenberg* (*Physics and Philosophy*, 1958): what we observe is not nature in itself but nature exposed to our method of questioning.
- *Born* (Nobel lecture, 1954): the statistical interpretation was forced by what scattering experiments actually yielded — theory bound to the measured.

**On observation over authority.**
- *Bacon* (*Novum Organum*, 1620): axioms must be drawn from experience by gradual induction, not invented or received from authority; to command nature one must first obey her.

- *Feynman* (Caltech commencement, 1974): the first principle is that you must not fool yourself, and you are the easiest person to fool.

**On theory corrected and vindicated by observation.**
- *Huxley* ("Biogenesis and Abiogenesis," 1870): the great tragedy of science is a beautiful hypothesis slain by an ugly fact.
- *Eddington* (*The Nature of the Physical World*, 1928): a theory's clash with observation is fatal however elegant the theory.

That is the consequence criterion of Section 7.2, and it is the criterion the empirical tradition has always, in practice, applied to its own generative laws.

## 9. Relation to the established corpus

We situate the construction against the genuine literature, distinguishing what is classical and established from what is the present contribution.

**9.1 Huygens–Fresnel–Kirchhoff.** Huygens (1690) gave secondary-wavelet sourcing as a propagation heuristic; Fresnel (1818) added phase and superposition; Kirchhoff (1882) gave the surface-integral foundation and the obliquity factor that suppresses the backward wave. The present paper uses the *forward envelope* and *stationary-phase* content of this classical apparatus and reads its off-envelope residue as entropy — an entropic reading of a propagation principle, which the optical tradition did not make because it was concerned with intensity, not information.

The link from Huygens summation to least action is itself classical and must be credited precisely. The optico-mechanical analogy — that constant-action surfaces propagate like wavefronts, connecting the rays of geometrical optics to the Huygens–Fresnel wavefront — was observed by Johann Bernoulli, developed by Maupertuis, and brought to full form in Hamilton's optico-mechanical theory (1830s); it is the historical root of wave mechanics. Dirac ("The Lagrangian in Quantum Mechanics," Phys. Z. Sowjetunion **3**, 64, 1933) observed that the short-time quantum transition amplitude corresponds to exp(iS/ℏ) with S the classical action, and Feynman ("Space-Time Approach to Non-Relativistic Quantum Mechanics," Rev. Mod. Phys. **20**, 367, 1948; Feynman and Hibbs, *Quantum Mechanics and Path Integrals*, 1965) built this into the path integral: the amplitude is a coherent sum over all paths weighted by exp(iS/ℏ), paths near the stationary-action (classical) trajectory interfere constructively, and the least-action principle is recovered by stationary phase (Feynman, *Lectures on Physics*, Vol. II, Ch. 19). The path integral *is* the Huygens construction iterated — every point a secondary source, the amplitude the wavelet sum — so the chain Huygens → path integral → stationary phase → least action is established physics, used directly in Theorem 8 for the coherent projection.

What is **not** in this prior art is the other projection. The established lineage extracts least action from the coherent (stationary-phase) envelope; it does not read the *complementary* off-envelope residue as entropy, nor identify the two as projections of one process. The informal observation sometimes made — that Huygens' principle and the maximum-entropy principle are "connected" because "both sum over possibilities" — is an analogy, not a derivation: summation over alternatives is generic to wave mechanics and to statistical inference alike, and noting the parallel establishes no structural identity. Theorem 8 supplies what the analogy lacks: the *same* secondary-wavelet superposition decomposes into a coherent part whose stationary-phase envelope is least action (Feynman's content) and an incoherent part whose phase-decohered angular spread is the maximum-entropy distribution (Jaynes' content, Section 9.4). The unification is the projection split of a single spherical re-emission, not a resemblance between two separately-summed quantities — and that split, with dx₄/dt = ic supplying the physical re-emission, is the contribution.

**9.2 Hadamard's strict-Huygens problem.** Hadamard (1923) formalized the strong Huygens principle (dependence only on the characteristic conoid, no interior tail) and showed it holds for the wave equation only in odd spatial dimension; the classification of operators with the strict property is the Hadamard conjecture, still active (Günther, Berest, and others). We use this as a genuine constraint (Remark 5): the sharp construction is odd-dimensional. We do not overstate beyond it.

**9.3 Boltzmann's H-theorem.** Boltzmann (1872) proved dH/dt ≤ 0 for the Stosszahlansatz kinetic equation, giving an arrow from molecular-collision coarse-graining. Our Theorem 3 is structurally analogous — a coarse-grained monotone — but its coarse-graining is *spatial-angular* (the λ-patch resolution on the wavefront) rather than the molecular-chaos assumption. Like Boltzmann's, our arrow is a statement about a coarse-grained quantity and is time-asymmetric only because the coarse-graining (forward envelope) is. We are explicit (Section 1.2) that this is an arrow, not yet maximization.

**9.4 Jaynes' maximum-entropy inference.** Jaynes (1957) recast equilibrium statistical mechanics as constrained entropy maximization: the realized distribution is the one of maximum Shannon/Gibbs entropy subject to the known moment constraints, obtained by Lagrange multipliers. Theorem 6 is a direct application: with only normalization and isotropy as constraints, the maximizer is uniform on the sphere. Jaynes supplies precisely the formal apparatus that distinguishes (B) from (A), which is why the maximization step is rigorous rather than asserted.

**9.5 Jacobson's thermodynamic Einstein equations — the contrast case.** Jacobson (1995) derived the Einstein field equations from the Clausius relation δQ = T δS applied to local Rindler horizons, treating G_μν = 8πG T_μν as a thermodynamic equation of state. This is the canonical "gravity from entropy" result, and it is the right contrast: Jacobson's derivation *requires* a horizon (Rindler), a temperature (Unruh), and Newton's constant (it produces G as the constant of proportionality, fixed by the entropy density 1/4ℓ_P²). Our construction is the deliberate complement — entropy from spreading *without* horizon, temperature, or G — and so does *not* and cannot derive the Einstein equations; it stops at the kinematic and information-theoretic entropy. We claim no gravitational output. This is the honest boundary: the horizon-free route buys G-independence at the price of not reaching gravitation. The two routes are complementary, not competing.

**9.6 Radiation and photon entropy.** The entropy derived here is, physically, the entropy of spreading light, and it descends from the oldest line in the subject. Planck (1900–1906) derived the entropy of blackbody radiation; Einstein (1905) used the entropy of radiation — that it counts as though composed of independent quanta — as the argument for the light quantum itself, so the entropy of light is historically where the photon concept originates. For a beam at frequency ν the standard Bose–Einstein result is S(ν) = k_B[(⟨n⟩+1)ln(⟨n⟩+1) − ⟨n⟩ln⟨n⟩], with ⟨n⟩ the mean occupation. The entropy of a *single* photon is definition-dependent — average, intrinsic, and effective definitions coexist (van Enk and Nienhuis 1992; Kirwan 2004; Chen, Mo and Hu 2008), and a recent first-principles treatment (Yu et al. 2022) shows the competing definitions coincide at hν/T only in equilibrium. This definition-dependence is not a defect; it is the same observer-relativity recorded in Remark 8: the entropy of light is a property of the description available to a given observer, not an intrinsic scalar carried by the field. The present construction takes the *spreading* (angular-mode, coarse-grained) reading of that entropy — the receiver's entropy — and derives its growth and its maximality from dx₄/dt = ic, which the radiation-thermodynamics tradition did not do because it worked from the equilibrium occupation, not from the geometry of propagation.

**9.7 Entropy from expansion: the squeezed-state and expanding-geometry tradition, and what dx₄/dt = ic adds.** The proposition that an *expanding geometry* generates an entropy arrow through coarse-graining of a unitarily-evolving state is established, and the present construction must be situated against it precisely — both to acknowledge the shared mechanism and to isolate what is new.

*The shared mechanism.* Two bodies of work establish the structure used in Proposition 7. First, the cosmological squeezed-state entropy programme: Brandenberger, Mukhanov and Prokopec (1992), Prokopec (1993), Gasperini and Giovannini (Phys. Lett. B **301**, 334, 1993; Class. Quant. Grav. **10**, L133, 1993), and Kiefer, Polarski and Starobinsky (2000) compute the entropy of cosmological perturbations and relic gravitons. The field mode, stretched by cosmological expansion, undergoes a Bogoliubov/squeeze transformation — the expansion acting as a parametric pump — and the evolution is strictly unitary, so the fine-grained von Neumann entropy is constant. Entropy appears upon coarse-graining: tracing the pure squeezed state over the unobserved squeezing phase (equivalently, reducing in the occupation-number or coherent-state basis) yields a mixed state of positive, growing entropy. Second, the expanding-geometry vacuum programme: Alfinito, Mańka and Vitiello ("Vacuum structure for expanding geometry," Class. Quant. Grav. **17**, 93, 2000) show that in a de Sitter phase the state space splits into unitarily inequivalent representations of the canonical commutation relations, the time-evolution generator is the entropy operator, and the thermodynamic arrow aligns with the cosmological (expansion) arrow. In a discrete-dynamics setting, Arrighi and collaborators (2023) prove that reversible expansion rules force entropy growth without a past hypothesis. Across all three, the skeleton is identical to Proposition 7: unitary underneath, fine-grained entropy constant, the arrow produced by coarse-graining away phase information, with expansion as the engine.

A closely related observer-relative formulation appears in Tozzi and Peters ("Entropy Balance in the Expanding Universe," Entropy **21**, 406, 2019): in an expanding universe information is "diluted," a local observer detects a decreasing density of bits, and the perceived thermodynamic entropy rises accordingly, linked through the Bekenstein bound and Landauer's principle. This is the Shannon/information reading of expansion-entropy — entropy as the dilution of an observer's position information — and it is the cosmological counterpart of the Shannon-position-entropy identification of Remark 1′. The informal observation that an expanding sphere of light carries a growing Shannon entropy of photon position (the position probability spreading over 4πr², the locating information diluting as the sphere grows) circulates in the information-theory and cosmology literature in exactly this spirit; the present paper makes it precise and derives it, identifying the diluting position-entropy with the Boltzmann angular-mode count (Remark 1′), proving its growth (Theorem 3) and its maximality (Theorem 6), and supplying through dx₄/dt = ic the physical mechanism of the spreading that the cosmological versions obtain from an imposed scale factor.

*The distinct physical sources.* The mechanism is shared; the physics generating the spreading is not. In the cosmological squeezed-state work the entropy is the entropy of **particle production**: quanta created from the vacuum by a *time-dependent metric*, with the coarse-graining performed over the **squeezing phase of produced pairs**. It requires field quantization and a dynamical background. In the Vitiello programme the entropy is the **pair-entanglement of a two-mode squeezed vacuum** across unitarily inequivalent representations, again driven by the changing de Sitter metric and requiring the infinite degrees of freedom of QFT. The present construction requires none of this. Its entropy is the entropy of **geometric spreading** — the angular-mode count on the expanding McGucken Sphere, the off-envelope Huygens residue (Theorem 3, Theorem 8) — with the coarse-graining performed over the **off-envelope/which-path phase of secondary wavelets**. It needs no particle production, no dynamical metric, no field quantization, and no gravitational coupling: only the wavefront expanding at c and the mode-spacing λ. The cosmological and expanding-geometry results are statements about quantum fields in an expanding *universe*; the present result is a statement about wavefront propagation in flat space, gravity-free.

*What dx₄/dt = ic adds: the physical mechanism.* In the cosmological and expanding-geometry literature, the expansion is an *external given* — the scale factor a(t) of an assumed FRW or de Sitter background, put in by hand as the cosmological input. The squeezing rate, the particle-production rate, and hence the entropy-production rate are all set by ȧ/a, which the formalism does not derive; it is supplied by the cosmological model. The expansion is the pump, but nothing in these treatments says *why* the geometry expands or fixes the rate from first principles. The expansion is not an external scale factor but the spherically-symmetric advance of the fourth dimension at the velocity of light from every event (Postulate, Definition 1), and the rate is fixed — it is c, not a free function ȧ/a. **9.8 Free expansion, Boltzmann entropy of non-interacting ensembles, and radiation thermodynamics.** Two further traditions bear directly on the present construction, and neither connected its subject to Huygens' principle.

*Boltzmann entropy of a freely expanding non-interacting ensemble.* The proposition central to this paper — that a non-interacting ensemble, merely by spreading, exhibits a monotone increase of coarse-grained entropy — is established rigorously by Chakraborti, Dhar, Goldstein, Kundu and Lebowitz, "Entropy growth during free expansion of an ideal gas" (J. Phys. A: Math. Theor. **55**, 394002, 2022), building on the Boltzmann–Lebowitz–Goldstein typicality programme (Lebowitz 1993; Goldstein and Lebowitz, Physica D **193**, 53, 2004; Goldstein, Lebowitz, Tumulka and Zanghì on Gibbs and Boltzmann entropy, 2020). Their result is that for a freely expanding gas of non-interacting point particles, the Boltzmann entropy S_B(t) of a typical microstate increases monotonically toward its maximum, that this requires neither ergodicity nor chaos nor interaction, and that the operative ingredients are initial conditions, typicality, large numbers, and coarse-graining. Two features coincide exactly with the present construction. First, the mechanism is identical to Proposition 7: reversible non-interacting dynamics, entropy increase produced by coarse-graining, no collisions required. Second — a quantitative correspondence — they find the rate of approach to maximum depends on the coarse-graining scale Δv, vanishing as Δv → 0 at fixed t; this is precisely the role of the mode-spacing λ in Theorem 3, where the entropy depends on λ while the *rate* structure is fixed by the coarse-graining scale. Their system is a one-dimensional gas of massive point particles in a box; the present system is the angular distribution on an expanding wavefront in three-space. The shared content is the coarse-grained entropy of a spreading non-interacting ensemble; the difference is that their spreading is the ballistic free flight of massive particles in a container, while the present spreading is the Huygens re-emission of a wavefront generated by dx₄/dt = ic. They do not invoke Huygens' principle: their ensemble is particles in position-velocity space, not wavelets on a wavefront, and the connection between free-expansion Boltzmann entropy and the Huygens construction is not made.

*Radiation thermodynamics.* The entropy of non-equilibrium radiation has its own line: Rosen (Phys. Rev. **96**, 555, 1954), Ore (Phys. Rev. **98**, 887, 1955), Petela (J. Heat Transfer **86**, 187, 1964), and Landsberg and Tonge (J. Appl. Phys. **51**, R1, 1980), with the exergy-of-radiation and effective-photon-temperature programme continued by Chen, Mo and Hu (2008) and the radiation-entropy spectroscopy of recent work (2017). This tradition computes the entropy of radiation that is out of equilibrium and the irreversible entropy production in radiative processes. But the entropy production it studies arises from *absorption and emission* — radiation interacting with matter — not from the free geometric spreading of a wavefront, and it works from spectral and exergy quantities, never from the Huygens construction. The radiation-thermodynamics tradition has the entropy of light and its irreversibility through interaction; it does not have, and does not seek, the entropy of light through pure propagation, and it makes no contact with Huygens' principle.

*The common gap.* Across both traditions of this section, and the expansion-entropy and optical traditions of Sections 9.1 and 9.7, the same absence recurs: the Boltzmann free-expansion school has the coarse-grained entropy of a spreading non-interacting ensemble but in position-velocity space, not on a Huygens wavefront; the radiation thermodynamicists have the entropy of light but through interaction, not propagation; the cosmologists have entropy from expansion but from an imposed metric, not from Huygens sourcing; and the optical tradition has the Huygens wavefront but never reads it as entropy. No prior line connects the Huygens construction to the entropy arrow. The present paper makes that connection and supplies, through dx₄/dt = ic, the physical mechanism of the spreading that each of the entropy-bearing traditions either assumed, imposed, or obtained from a different physical system.

**9.9 The radiation arrow and Wheeler–Feynman absorber theory — the nearest prior art.** The closest existing connection between outward spherical-wave propagation and time-asymmetry is the *radiation arrow*: the fact that one observes diverging (retarded) spherical waves from a source and never the time-reverse, a converging wave that focuses inward upon it. This is the prior work the present construction sits nearest to, and it must be credited precisely.

Wheeler and Feynman ("Interaction with the Absorber as the Mechanism of Radiation," Rev. Mod. Phys. **17**, 157, 1945; "Classical Electrodynamics in Terms of Direct Interparticle Action," Rev. Mod. Phys. **21**, 425, 1949), building on Tetrode, Ritz, and Fokker, addressed why the time-symmetric wave equation — whose retarded and advanced solutions are equally valid — yields only outgoing radiation in practice. Their resolution ties the asymmetry to an absorber boundary condition, with a statistical underpinning explicitly *analogous to Boltzmann's H-theorem*: complete forward absorption is overwhelmingly probable, its time-reverse overwhelmingly improbable. The standard sharpening of the radiation arrow makes the point in exactly the form used in this paper: a diverging spherical wave is generated by a localized source event, whereas the converging branch requires phase coordination across the entire wavefront to sub-wavelength precision — a measure-zero condition in the space of physically prepared states. This is precisely the argument invoked in Proposition 7(iii) for why the spreading branch is realized and the focusing branch is not; the present paper does not claim originality for it, and credits the radiation-arrow / Wheeler–Feynman lineage as its source.

What the radiation-arrow tradition supplies is the *direction* of the arrow — which spherical solution is realized, and why its reverse is improbable. What it does not supply, and what the present construction adds, is fourfold, each item stated as a distinct increment:

1. *A quantitative coarse-grained entropy on the expanding wavefront.* Wheeler–Feynman give an H-theorem-*like* probability argument for absorption; they do not construct a state function S(t) increasing along the propagation. The present paper does: S(t) = k_B ln N(t) = k_B ln(A(t)/λ²) (Definition 4, Theorem 3), with an explicit rate dS/dt = 2k_B/(t−t₀).

2. *The Shannon-information identification.* The radiation arrow is posed in terms of fields and absorbers, not information. The present paper identifies S(t) exactly with the Shannon entropy of the photon's position distribution on the wavefront (Remark 1′), making the arrow a statement about the dilution of locating-information.

3. *The least-action/entropy projection split.* The radiation-arrow tradition treats the outgoing wave as a single object. The present paper decomposes the one wavefront amplitude into a coherent projection (least action, Theorem 8(i), Theorem 6.2.2(i)) and an incoherent projection (maximum entropy, Theorem 8(ii), Theorem 6.2.2(ii)), exhibiting least action and the second law as two projections of one spreading process — a structure absent from the radiation-arrow literature.

4. *A physical generator of the expansion.* In Wheeler–Feynman the outgoing wave is the retarded Green's function of the wave equation, taken as given; the spherical spreading is not derived from a principle. The present paper supplies the generator: the wavefront is the McGucken Sphere expanding at rate c from every event by dx₄/dt = ic (Definition 1), fixing the rate and the geometry from a single physical principle rather than positing the retarded solution.

The relation is therefore one of genuine inheritance and genuine extension: the present construction takes the radiation arrow's measure-zero/phase-coordination account of *why spreading is realized* (Proposition 7) and builds upon it a quantitative, information-theoretic, projection-resolved entropy generated by dx₄/dt = ic. The radiation arrow answers "which way"; this paper answers "how much," "of what information," "alongside which other principle," and "generated how."

**Remark 9.9.1 (The direction of explanation: second law as output, not input).** The deepest structural difference is the direction in which the explanation runs, and it bears directly on a circularity in the Wheeler–Feynman account. There, the radiation arrow is reduced to the absorber condition, the absorber condition to the second law, and the second law — on the standard Boltzmann–Lebowitz reading — to the past hypothesis, the posited low-entropy initial state of the universe. No arrow in that chain is independently generated; each is explained by another, and the chain terminates in an assumed boundary condition. The present construction inverts this. The entropy is generated by the geometry of the spreading itself: N(t) = A(t)/λ² grows because the McGucken Sphere's area grows, and the area grows because dx₄/dt = ic drives the expansion at rate c (Theorem 3). The second law is therefore an *output* here (Theorem 6, the maximization), not an input; it is not assumed in order to explain the spreading, but derived from the spreading. No absorber and no future boundary condition appear.

**Remark 9.9.2 (The low-entropy initial state is generic, not posited; and the arrow is derived from the principle's one-way advance).** Two further points sharpen the contrast. First, the low-entropy initial state is not a fine-tuned stipulation in this construction but the generic starting configuration: every McGucken Sphere is generated from a point event, where at t = t₀ the radius is zero, the mode count N(t₀) is at its floor, and the entropy S = k_B ln N is minimal. "Born from a point and expanding" is precisely what dx₄/dt = ic asserts at every event, so the entropy floor at origin is automatic, not assumed — in contrast to the global past hypothesis, which posits a special low-entropy macrostate for the universe as a whole. Second, and decisively, the arrow is *derived from the principle*. dx₄/dt = ic is a physical principle: the fourth dimension advances at the velocity of light, one-way, from every event. From this the arrow follows as a theorem: the McGucken Sphere expands rather than contracts because x₄ advances; N(t) therefore grows; the entropy S(t) = k_B ln N(t) therefore increases (Theorem 3); and the thermodynamic, radiative, measurement, and cosmological arrows are all the one forward x₄-advance read in different sectors. The economy over Wheeler–Feynman is total: they require a time-symmetric law *plus* an independently-posited absorber/past-hypothesis asymmetry — two inputs, the second ungrounded — whereas the present construction requires only the principle dx₄/dt = ic, from which the expansion, the entropy increase, and every arrow descend as consequences. The second law is the principle's output, not its premise; the low-entropy start is the principle's generic initial state, not a posit; and the arrow is the principle's forward advance.
