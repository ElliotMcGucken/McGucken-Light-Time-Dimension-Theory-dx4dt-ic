### 11.1 The null cone is the spherical expansion at c (Theorem 11.1)

> **Theorem 11.1 (The causal structure is the expansion at c).** *Let (V,η) be the observed Lorentzian spacetime and p₀ = (x₀, t₀) any event. *Proof.* The null condition η(p−p₀,p−p₀)=0 reads −c²(t−t₀)² + |x−x₀|² = 0, hence |x−x₀| = c|t−t₀|; the future branch gives the stated sphere, with ṙ = d[c(t−t₀)]/dt = c (verified symbolically). ### 13.9 What Donaldson and His Colleagues Claimed, and What Really Happened

The lineage of §13.1–13.3 is a statement about mathematical and historical fact. This subsection sets that fact against the *self-understanding* of the mathematical community — what Donaldson and his colleagues said they had done — because the gap between the two is itself evidence for the framework's reading. The claims are documented and quoted; the contrast is then stated at grade. The pattern is uniform: the result was presented as a *topology* theorem reached by *importing a tool from physics*, with the metric and the imaginary unit treated as auxiliary scaffolding, and the effectiveness of the physics regarded as a *surprise*. Under the framework's reading (Theorem 13.6), there is no surprise to explain: the topology was being decided by the frozen form of the single i of dx₄/dt = ic, used at every load-bearing step and named only "Riemannian." The recorded astonishment is the exact measure of the unnamed identification.

**The claim: a topology result importing an external physical tool.** The framing begins with the paper's own title. Donaldson's 1982/1983 result was published as *"Self-dual connections and the topology of smooth 4-manifolds"* (Bulletin of the AMS, 1983) and in full as *"An application of gauge theory to four-dimensional topology"* (Journal of Differential Geometry 18, 279–315, 1983). Both titles encode the same self-description: an *application of* gauge theory *to* topology — a tool borrowed from one discipline, a result delivered in another. Nothing in the framing suggests the gauge theory is anything but an external instrument, or that the metric on which it operates is primary.

The community's reception sharpened this into an explicit narrative of *surprise* — the claim that physics' equations had no antecedent business proving theorems of smooth topology:

- **Atiyah** (Donaldson's doctoral supervisor), on the impact of the paper, is recorded as saying it "stunned the mathematical world." The word is one of shock at an unanticipated intrusion of one field into another.
- **Manolescu** (Stanford four-dimensional topology survey) records that Donaldson's work introduced an unexpected tool in smooth 4-dimensional topology: gauge theory. The operative word is *unexpected* — the tool is framed as foreign to the subject it cracked.
- **Iga** (2002 survey, *"What do topologists want from Seiberg–Witten theory?"*, Int. J. Mod. Phys. A) writes that in 1983 Donaldson shocked the topology world by using instantons from physics to prove new theorems about four-manifolds. The framing is cross-disciplinary astonishment: instantons are *from physics*, deployed in topology.

The physical origin of the tool was openly stated, never hidden — which is exactly why it was experienced as surprising rather than as intrinsic. The instantons are described as a particular solution to the equations of Yang–Mills gauge theory which has its origin in quantum field theory. Lawson's 1983 CBMS lectures (*Theory of Gauge Fields in Four Dimensions*, AMS 1985), the first expository account, characterized the episode as one mathematical discipline yielding startling consequences in another.

So the claim, uniformly across the principals and the early literature, is: *two separate disciplines; astonishingly, the physical one settled a problem in the topological one.* At no point did anyone claim the imaginary unit in the construction was the relativistic i, that the metric was doing the decisive work, or that the topology was in any sense already physical. The metric and the gauge i were scaffolding — auxiliary, to be erected for the proof and then, ideally, removed.

**What really happened: the physics was not removable, and the surprise was the missing identification.** The "borrowed external tool" account makes a prediction it cannot meet: if gauge theory were merely a clever instrument, it should be *dispensable* once the topological result is secured. The subsequent history is the precise refutation, in three documented stages.

*Stage 1 — the invariants are a quantum field theory (Witten, 1988).* Far from removable, the gauge theory turned out to *be* the subject. Witten showed the Donaldson invariants are correlation functions of a topological quantum field theory — a twisted 𝒩=2 super-Yang–Mills theory. In Witten's 1994 statement of the established fact, Donaldson theory is equivalent to a quantum field theory, in fact, a twisted version of N=2 supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory. The topology was not *using* a QFT; it *was* a QFT in topological language.

*Stage 2 — removing one piece of physics requires another (Seiberg–Witten, 1994).* When the instanton machinery was finally dispensed with, it was replaced not by topology but by *different physics*. ## 15. Geometric Langlands and the Same Single i: Apparatus, Lineage, and the Sociology of a Delayed Recognition

The lineage established for Donaldson (§13, §14) is not isolated. The same single complex structure — the connection-and-curvature apparatus carrying the i of dx₄/dt=ic — is the operative machinery of the geometric Langlands correspondence, present and load-bearing in its 1980s–1990s construction, and recognized as the S-duality of a four-dimensional quantum field theory only in 2006. This section establishes that parallel with the same dual rigor: the mathematics and physics of the apparatus, and the history and sociology of its delayed recognition. The honest boundary is marked as before — the arithmetic core of Langlands (the Galois–automorphic correspondence) is i-free and is not generated by the primitive — and the section closes by naming the methodological fallacy that the delayed recognition exemplifies, with an analogy that makes the fallacy unmistakable.

### 15.1 The Wright analogy: the fallacy of origin-by-date

A recurring error in reading these lineages is to suppose that because a foundational principle was articulated earlier and in simpler form, the later and more elaborate developments are not instances of it. The error is exhibited cleanly by an analogy. The Wright brothers achieved powered, controlled, fixed-wing flight in 1903; they died decades before the jet engine. It would be a plain fallacy to conclude that jet airliners "have nothing to do with" the Wrights — that because the Wrights predate jets and flew something far simpler, jets are a separate matter. On the contrary: every jet is powered controlled fixed-wing flight, the very thing the Wrights established, developed further. The principle is continuous; the later machine does not escape the origin by being more elaborate or by post-dating its originators. We name this the **origin-by-date fallacy**: inferring discontinuity of *principle* from succession of *date*.

The fallacy has appeared twice already and was corrected each time. Cartan's connection–curvature apparatus is not disqualified as the carrier of the single i by the fact that his *earliest* (pre-1908) work predates Minkowski (§13, Remark 13.7): the relevant apparatus is the 1922–25 work, and in any case the *single i* it carries is one object across all its appearances (Theorem 5.6), not a different i at each date. Likewise here: the single i in geometric Langlands is not a different i from the relativistic one merely because the curve, the D-module, and the moduli stack are complex-algebraic constructions bearing later names. It is one i, the relativistic one, developed further — the jet, not a separate craft.

## 19. Conclusion: One Operation

The interval is quartic in the spinor (Thm 3.3); its bilinear square is null (Thm 3.2, 5.1); the null cone is the x₄-rest locus (ontology ii); the tensor square of the spinor is the exterior algebra of the x₄-arena (Thm 4.1); the 4π period is one circuit of the x₄-extended S³ with the sign parked in the x₄ scalar (Thm 7.1); and the single i doing all of this is the i of dx₄/dt = ic (Prop 6.1). Dirac extracted the square root of Klein–Gordon by introducing γ^μ — operators encoding the fourth axis. Atiyah called the spinor the square root of geometry and could not say, geometrically, of *what*.

> Dirac's square root of Klein–Gordon and Atiyah's square root of geometry are the **same operation** — the extraction of the x₄ axis. The spinor is the fourth root of the McGucken interval, and what it ultimately square-roots is the x₄-stationary null cone: the geometric meaning of √−1, made physical as dx₄/dt = ic.

Atiyah's two mysteries — √−1 and the spinor — are not two. They are the one i, seen twice.

And the magnitude is not free. The spinor's null cone and the x₄-expansion are a single constant entering a single interval (Thm 5.4): the spinor's lightcone pins the expansion to c, and were x₄ not expanding at c the spinor's bilinear square would lie on another cone — it would not be the object it is. Spinors and the motion of x₄ at c are mutually determined; that is the one place in this development where the motion itself, beyond the fourth axis, does the work.

Beneath the magnitude lies the signature. A single-complex spinor — one i, one Hermitian norm — forces a four-space of signature (1,3), exactly one moving dimension, because its bilinear lives in the Hermitian 2×2 matrices whose only invariant form is the Lorentzian determinant (Thm 5.6). The timelike direction is the Born norm ⟨ψ|ψ⟩, built from the same i that is x₄ = ict. A fully static four-space is the quaternionic SU(2) × SU(2) world, in which that single i is replaced by an anticommuting triple and the complex spinor is no longer present. So the deepest reading of the question is exact: one i, one moving dimension — the imaginary unit of the wavefunction and the motion of x₄ are not two things.

And the same one i reaches the statistics. Exchange is a 2π rotation (Prop 8.1); a 2π rotation is (−1)^{2s} (Lemma 8.2); and that sign is, by Theorem 7.1, a half-circuit through x₄ (Theorem 8.3). Pauli exclusion becomes the cancellation of states carrying opposite x₄ half-circuit phase, and the fermion/boson split becomes the single-i/quaternionic split of Theorem 5.6 (Proposition 8.4) — a reason of the kind Duck and Sudarshan found absent from the analyticity-of-VEV proofs, contingent on the two open problems we have isolated. The square root, the signature, the rotation, and the statistics are one fact about dx₄/dt = ic, viewed at successive depths.

Finally, the double cover that underlies all of this — the half-angle, the 2π ↦ −1 — is the same square root once more (§9). The spatial rotation is the conjugation-square of the x₄-carrying transport (Lemma 9.1), just as the vector is the bilinear-square of the spinor (§3.2): one root, two faces, the observable world blind to the x₄-scalar that is the root's sign (Prop 9.3). Granting Theorem 5.6, the double cover is derived (Thm 9.4), and Atiyah's question narrows to its irreducible core — not what the spinor *is* (the fourth root of the x₄-interval) nor why it doubles (the conjugation square), but why geometry should be founded on its square root at all. In Atiyah's own words this is *what is a spinor when there is no complex structure* — which in Light Time Dimension is *what is geometry with no moving dimension* — and the program he named for answering it, a "spinor analysis" standing to the spinor as complex analysis stands to √−1, is in Light Time Dimension the calculus of the first-order x₄-generator (Prop 2.1) whose exponential is motion. That the rate is primitive and the coordinate its integrated shadow is, at last, the same statement as Atiyah's that the first-order operator is fundamental because it exponentiates to motion. That is the one question dx₄/dt = ic has not yet answered, and the one worth the next monograph.

And the static/moving split has a striking corollary in four-manifold geometry. Donaldson's exotic dimension four — the diagonalization theorem, the gauge invariants, the uncountable smooth structures on ℝ⁴ — lives in the definite (4,0) sector, which by Theorem 10.2 contains no relativity, no quantum mechanics, and not the Lorentz or unitary symmetries either requires: the most exotic *static* geometry known, and physically inert. The expansion does not manufacture dimension four's topology — that is a static gift — but it is what makes dimension four *physical*, what turns Donaldson's frozen arena into a spacetime that can hold relativity, quantum mechanics, and the physical Dirac spinor of Atiyah. The square root, the signature, the rotation, the statistics, the cone's nonlocality, and the injection of physics into the static four-space are all one fact about dx₄/dt = ic, read at successive depths.
