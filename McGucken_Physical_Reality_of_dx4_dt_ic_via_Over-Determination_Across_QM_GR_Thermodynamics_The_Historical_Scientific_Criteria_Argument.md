## Why the Question Is Not Trivial

A naive answer is that physical reality is settled by experiment, full
stop. This is a partial view of how the discipline has actually
operated.

No foundational principle of physics has ever been "proven" by
direct experiment alone. Newton's second law F = ma cannot be
falsified by any single measurement; one always has the option of
positing an unobserved force. Special relativity was not established
by Michelson--Morley alone --- the null result is consistent with
several alternatives (Lorentz's ether contraction, Ritz's emission
theory). General relativity was not established by the perihelion of
Mercury alone --- Newcomb's modified-gravity formula also fits the
44 arcsecond residue. Quantum mechanics was not established by any
one experiment --- the Bohr model fit hydrogen but failed for helium,
and matrix mechanics and wave mechanics had to be shown equivalent
before either was secure.

What established each of these as a foundational physical principle
was the *convergence of multiple independent lines of evidence*,
both empirical and structural, on a single underlying claim. The
specific form of this convergence has a name in the philosophy of
science: William Whewell's *consilience of inductions*, the
principle that "the induction obtained from one class of facts
coincides with an induction obtained from another different class.
This consilience is a test of the truth of the theory in which it
occurs" [@Whewell1840]. Einstein expressed essentially the same
criterion when he distinguished "constructive theories," built from
particular hypotheses, from "principle theories," justified by their
power to coordinate disparate empirical structures under a single
postulate [@Einstein1919].

The convergence is not Bayesian arithmetic alone. It has a
*structural* component that is older than the Bayesian
formalism: when independent derivational routes from a candidate
principle converge on the same empirical equation, the agreement is
evidence for the principle in a way that no single derivation can be.
This is what is meant, in classical philosophy of science, by saying
that a theory is *over-determined* by the evidence.

The McGucken corpus has, by the count of the present paper, produced
over-determination of the standard equations of physics through
structurally disjoint derivational chains, recovered the empirical
symmetries and Lagrangians as theorems rather than postulates, and
satisfied the falsifiability and parsimony criteria at a standard

the paper makes this case in detail.

::: {#crit:predict .crit}
**Criterion 1** (Predictive Success). *A candidate principle must reproduce, as deductive consequences, the
empirically successful predictions of the theories it would replace
or subsume. Failure on this criterion is fatal: no principle is
admitted as foundational if it contradicts a verified empirical
result.*
:::

**Criterion 2** (Derivational Over-Determination). *A foundational principle is supported when the same empirical
equation is derivable from it through two or more structurally

This is the criterion of *consilience of inductions* in
Whewell's sense [@Whewell1840].*
:::

::: {#crit:coincidence .crit}
**Criterion 3** (Recovery of Coincidental Identities). *A foundational principle is supported when previously unexplained

independently for different purposes --- are recovered as forced

:::

::: {#crit:unification .crit}
**Criterion 4** (Unification Across Regimes). *A foundational principle is supported when it unifies regimes of
physics that the prior framework treated as independent. Newton's
universal gravitation unified terrestrial and celestial mechanics;
Maxwell unified electricity, magnetism, and optics; the Standard

:::

::: {#crit:parsimony .crit}
**Criterion 6** (Kolmogorov--Complexity Parsimony). *Other criteria being satisfied, the candidate principle with the
smaller information content is preferred. The bit-cost of specifying
the principle must be compared with the bit-cost of specifying the
collection of theories it would replace. This is the rigorous form
of Occam's razor; in physics it has historically taken the form of
preferring the principle that subsumes the largest number of prior
postulates under the smallest formal statement.*
:::

::: {#crit:falsifiability .crit}
**Criterion 7** (Falsifiability with Unbroken Record). *A foundational principle must specify, in advance, observations or
structural results that would refute it; and the empirical record at
the moment of evaluation must contain no instance of such refuting
observations. Popper's criterion [@Popper1959] is necessary but
not sufficient: a principle is not established merely by being
falsifiable. What is required is that the empirical record covering
the full domain of the principle's applicability contain no
falsifying observation.*
:::

::: {#crit:convergent .crit}
**Criterion 8** (Convergent (Whewellian--Bayesian) Consilience). *A foundational principle is established when the joint probability,
under the hypothesis that the principle is correct, of the observed
pattern of independent structural alignments and empirical
corroborations is order-unity, while the joint probability of the
same pattern under the null hypothesis (coincidence) is vanishingly
small. This is the Bayesian formalisation of Whewell's consilience
[@Whewell1840; @Howson1989; @Sober2008]. Its application requires that
the alignments be genuinely independent, that the principle be
sharply specified, and that no alignment be a definitional
restatement of another.*
:::

## What Is Not Claimed

We state, with the same precision as the positive claim, what the
present paper does not claim.

It does not claim that dx_4/dt = ic has been proven in the

No foundational principle of physics has ever been so proven; the
notion does not apply.

It does not claim that the convergent evidence licenses the framework
against future refutation. The framework remains falsifiable at the
five levels F1--F5 of §; if a future

It does not claim that the McGucken corpus has answered every open
question in foundational physics. The cosmological constant, the

What is claimed is the eighth criterion's conclusion: the convergent

epistemic class as the foundational principles of physics that
