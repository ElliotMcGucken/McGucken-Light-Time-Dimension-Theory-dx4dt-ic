# 6.0.1 Chern Classes (Deligne 2006 Remark (ii)) Through the Corpus Gauge-Group Derivation

## 6.0.1.1 Deligne's verbatim statement and what it asserts

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Verbatim (Deligne 2006 \S 2 Remark (ii)).}\enspace 

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{``On a projective non-singular variety $X$ over $\mathbb{C}$, the group of integral linear combinations of classes $\mathrm{cl}(Z)$ of algebraic cycles coincides with the group of integral linear combinations of products of Chern classes of algebraic (equivalently by GAGA: analytic) vector bundles. To express $\mathrm{cl}(Z)$ in terms of Chern classes, one resolves the structure sheaf $\mathcal{O}_Z$ by a finite complex of vector bundles. That Chern classes are algebraic cycles holds, basically, because vector bundles have plenty of meromorphic sections.''}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What Remark (ii) asserts.}\enspace 
The map
$$\{\text{algebraic vector bundles } E \to X\} \;\xrightarrow{\;\text{Chern character}\;}\; H^{2*}(X, \mathbb{Q})$$
has image in the algebraic-cycle classes, and the image generates (over $\mathbb{Z}$, via integer linear combinations of products) the full subgroup of $H^{2*}(X, \mathbb{Z})$ spanned by algebraic-cycle classes $\mathrm{cl}(Z)$. The construction is: for a codimension-$p$ algebraic cycle $Z \subset X$, resolve the structure sheaf $\mathcal{O}_Z$ by a finite complex of algebraic vector bundles
$$0 \longrightarrow E_n \longrightarrow E_{n-1} \longrightarrow \cdots \longrightarrow E_0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_Z \longrightarrow 0,$$
and express $\mathrm{cl}(Z) = \sum_i (-1)^i \mathrm{ch}(E_i) \cdot \mathrm{Td}(TX)^{-1}|_{2p}$ via the Grothendieck--Riemann--Roch theorem, with the right-hand side an integer combination of Chern-class products.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The foundational reading.}\enspace 
The Hodge conjecture, in the light of Remark (ii), is the question: \emph{which integer combinations of products of Chern classes of algebraic vector bundles are realized as Hodge classes (rationally)?} Algebraic vector bundles on a smooth complex projective variety are classified by the moduli space of holomorphic vector bundles (Narasimhan--Seshadri / GAGA); Chern classes are the curvature-invariants of $U(n)$-connections on these bundles via Chern--Weil theory. The Hodge conjecture is therefore centrally about \emph{which Chern-class products of $U(n)$-bundles on $X$ span $H^{p,p}(X) \cap H^{2p}(X, \mathbb{Q})$}.

# 6.0.2 K-Theory (Deligne 2006 Remark (iv)) Through the Corpus Atiyah--Singer / Dirac-Operator Content

## 6.0.2.1 Deligne's verbatim statement

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Verbatim (Deligne 2006 \S 2 Remark (iv)).}\enspace 

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{``The relation between algebraic cycles and algebraic vector bundles is also the basis of the Atiyah and Hirzebruch theorem [2] that the Hodge conjecture cannot hold integrally. In the Atiyah--Hirzebruch spectral sequence from cohomology to topological K-theory,}
$$E_2^{pq} \;=\; H^p(X, K^q(\mathrm{pt})) \;\Rightarrow\; K^{p+q}(X);$$
\emph{the resulting filtration of $K^n(X)$ is by the}
$$F^p K^n(X) \;=\; \ker\!\bigl(K^n(X) \;\to\; K^n((p - 1)\text{-skeleton, in any triangulation})\bigr).$$
\emph{Equivalently, a class $c$ is in $F^p$ if for some topological subspace $Y$ of codimension $p$, $c$ is the image of a class $\tilde c$ with support in $Y$. If $Z$ is an algebraic cycle of codimension $p$, a resolution of $\mathcal{O}_Z$ defines a K-theory class with support in $Z$: $c_Z \in K^0(X, X - Z)$. Its image in $F^p K^0(X)$ agrees with the class of $Z$ in $H^{2p}(X, \mathbb{Z})$. The latter hence is in the kernel of the successive differentials $d_r$ of the spectral sequence.}

\emph{No counterexample is known to the statement that integral $(p, p)$ classes killed by all $d_r$ are integral linear combinations of classes $\mathrm{cl}(Z)$. One has no idea of which classes should be effective, that is, of the form $\mathrm{cl}(Z)$, rather than a difference of such.''}

## 6.0.2.2 What Remark (iv) asserts

The Atiyah--Hirzebruch spectral sequence $E_2^{pq} = H^p(X, K^q(\mathrm{pt})) \Rightarrow K^{p+q}(X)$ converges to the topological K-theory $K^*(X)$ from the singular cohomology of $X$ with coefficients in the K-theory of a point. The differentials $d_r : E_r^{p,q} \to E_r^{p+r, q-r+1}$ are integer-cohomology obstructions to lifting integer cohomology classes to K-theory classes; the surviving $E_\infty^{p,q}$ pieces are the associated graded of the filtration $F^p K^{p+q}(X)$.

An algebraic cycle $Z \subset X$ of codimension $p$ supplies, via the resolution of $\mathcal{O}_Z$ by a finite complex of algebraic vector bundles (Deligne Remark (ii); also \S 6.0.1.1 of this supplement), a K-theory class $c_Z \in K^0(X, X-Z)$ with support in $Z$. The image of $c_Z$ in $F^p K^0(X) \subset K^0(X)$, after the spectral-sequence convergence, agrees with $\mathrm{cl}(Z) \in H^{2p}(X, \mathbb{Z})$. The differentials $d_r$ therefore kill $\mathrm{cl}(Z)$: every algebraic-cycle class is in the kernel of every $d_r$.

The Atiyah--Hirzebruch integral failure of the Hodge conjecture: there exist integer $(p,p)$-classes that are \emph{not} killed by some $d_r$, hence are not images of algebraic-cycle K-theory classes. The Atiyah--Hirzebruch counterexample is on a $7$-dimensional Stiefel manifold; subsequent counterexamples by Kollár (1992) and Voisin (2007) work on Calabi--Yau threefolds and smooth projective varieties.

Deligne's open question (Remark (iv) sentence 2): are the integer $(p, p)$-classes killed by all $d_r$ exactly the integer combinations of $\mathrm{cl}(Z)$? No counterexample is known.

# 6.0.3 Griffiths Transversality (Deligne 2006 §1) Through the Family-Variation of the $J^2 = -1$ Holomorphicity Structure

## 6.0.3.1 Deligne's verbatim statement

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Verbatim (Deligne 2006 \S 1).}\enspace 

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{``When $X$ moves in a holomorphic family, the Hodge filtration $F^p := \bigoplus_{a \geq p} H^{a, n-a}$ of $H^n(X, \mathbb{C})$ is better behaved than the Hodge decomposition. Locally on the parameter space $T$, $H^n(X_t, \mathbb{C})$ is independent of $t \in T$ and the Hodge filtration can be viewed as a variable filtration $F(t)$ on a fixed vector space. It varies holomorphically with $t$, and obeys Griffiths transversality: at first order around $t_0 \in T$, $F^p(t)$ remains in $F^{p-1}(t_0)$.''}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What Griffiths transversality asserts.}\enspace 
For a holomorphic family $\pi : \mathcal{X} \to T$ of smooth complex projective varieties (the fibres $X_t = \pi^{-1}(t)$), the Hodge filtration $F^p$ varies holomorphically with $t \in T$, with the additional first-order constraint
$$\partial_t F^p(t)|_{t = t_0} \;\subset\; F^{p-1}(t_0)$$
(``$F^p$ moves into $F^{p-1}$ at first order, and no further''). This is the foundational constraint on variations of Hodge structure (Griffiths 1968, 1970), used throughout the literature including in Cattani--Deligne--Kaplan 1995 [Deligne 2006 ref [4]] for the algebraicity of the locus of Hodge classes.

## 6.0.3.2 The framework's holomorphicity content

Master Monograph Definition 3.1 fixes the $+i$ orientation of the local McGucken Sphere $\Sigma_M(x)$ from the McGucken Axiom $dx_4/dt = +ic$: the Sphere expands outward along the $+i$ direction of the imaginary axis, and this orientation transports to the local complexified tangent space via the canonical identification of the $J$-action on $TX$ with the $\mathbb{C}^*$-action of Deligne \S 1 (Master Monograph \S 2.1).

The just-shipped \emph{[Master-Monograph-Supplement-II]} (June 8, 2026, insertable as \S 3.2.5) establishes that the local Hodge $(p, q)$-form components are the $i^{p - q}$-eigenspaces of the wedge-extended $J^*$-action on $\bigwedge^k T_x^* X \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C}$, with the bigrading reading
$$(p, q) \;=\; (\text{forward-}x_4 \text{ Sphere modes},\; \text{backward-}x_4 \text{ Sphere modes}).$$

The framework therefore supplies:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(H1) Local $J^* = \pm i$-eigenspace decomposition.}\enspace At each smooth point $x$ of each fibre $X_t$, the cotangent space $T_x^* X_t \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C}$ decomposes into $\pm i$-eigenspaces of $J^*$, with the $(p, q)$-bigrading on wedge powers given by the $i^{p - q}$-eigenvalue decomposition (\emph{[Master-Monograph-Supplement-II]} Theorem 3.2.5.1).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(H2) Holomorphic structure.}\enspace The $+i$-eigenspace of $J^*$ is the holomorphic cotangent space $T_x^{*(1,0)} X_t$. The differential of a holomorphic function $f : X_t \to \mathbb{C}$ at $x$ lies in $T_x^{*(1,0)} X_t$ (the $+i$-eigenspace) iff $f$ is holomorphic, i.e., $\bar\partial f = 0$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(H3) The family-variation step.}\enspace The Hodge filtration $F^p$ on $H^n(X_t, \mathbb{C})$ is defined globally on $X_t$ from the local-cotangent decomposition (H1) via the harmonic-form-on-Kähler-class isomorphism and the Hodge decomposition. As $t$ varies in $T$, the family-variation of $F^p(t)$ is the family-variation of the global integral of the local $J^* = \pm i$-eigenspace decomposition on each $X_t$.

## 6.0.3.3 Reading 6.0.3.1 (Griffiths Transversality as Family-Variation of the $J$-Structure)

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Reading 6.0.3.1 (Griffiths Transversality in Framework Vocabulary).}\enspace 
\emph{The Griffiths transversality condition $\partial_t F^p(t)|_{t_0} \subset F^{p-1}(t_0)$ is, in the McGucken framework, the family-variation statement of the $J^2 = -1$ holomorphicity structure (H1)--(H2): as the complex structure $J_t$ varies with $t$ (the family-variation of $J$), the $\pm i$-eigenspaces of $J_t^*$ on the cotangent bundle vary in a constrained way, with the constraint at first order being that the variation moves a $(p, q)$-form to a $(p-1, q+1)$-form plus a $(p+1, q-1)$-form, hence stays within $F^{p-1}$.}

\medskip
\noindent The content: Griffiths transversality is the constraint that the family-variation of the $J$-structure on the tangent bundle is itself constrained by the wedge-power compatibility of the cotangent variation. The constraint at first order:
$$\partial_t J_t|_{t_0} : T_x X_{t_0} \to T_x X_{t_0} \quad\text{with}\quad J_{t_0} \partial_t J_t|_{t_0} + \partial_t J_t|_{t_0} J_{t_0} = 0,$$
i.e., $\partial_t J_t$ is anti-commuting with $J_{t_0}$. This is the Kodaira--Spencer first-order deformation condition for the complex structure (Kodaira--Spencer 1958; reviewed in Voisin 2002 Vol.\ II \S 9), and it produces the Griffiths-transversality phenomenon at the cotangent-wedge-power level.

## 6.0.3.4 Theorem 6.0.3.1 (Griffiths Transversality from Family-Variation of $J$-Structure)

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Theorem 6.0.3.1 (Griffiths Transversality as Family-Variation of \emph{[Master-Monograph-Supplement-II]} Theorem 3.2.5.1).}\enspace 
\emph{Let $\pi : \mathcal{X} \to T$ be a holomorphic family of smooth complex projective varieties with fibres $X_t$. By \emph{[Master-Monograph-Supplement-II]} Theorem 3.2.5.1, at each smooth point $x \in X_t$ the local Hodge $(p, q)$-bigrading is the $i^{p - q}$-eigenspace decomposition of the wedge-extended $J_t^*$-action on $\bigwedge^k T_x^* X_t \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C}$. The family-variation of $J_t$ in $t \in T$ satisfies the Kodaira--Spencer anti-commutation $J_{t_0} \partial_t J_t|_{t_0} + \partial_t J_t|_{t_0} J_{t_0} = 0$, which forces the family-variation of the $i^{p-q}$-eigenspaces of $J_t^*$ to satisfy the Griffiths transversality condition at first order: $\partial_t F^p(t)|_{t_0} \subset F^{p-1}(t_0)$.}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Proof (rigor level \textsc{r2}-strict).}\enspace 

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Step 1: Family-variation of $J_t$.}\enspace By the Kodaira--Spencer theory of deformations of complex structures (Kodaira--Spencer 1958, recapitulated in Voisin 2002 Vol.\ II \S 9.1), a smooth family of complex structures $J_t$ on a fixed smooth manifold $M$ corresponds locally to a smooth family of $(1, 0)$-distributions in $TM \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C}$, with the integrability of the distribution at each $t$ characterizing the complex structure $J_t$. The infinitesimal variation $\partial_t J_t|_{t_0}$ anti-commutes with $J_{t_0}$ because $J_t^2 = -1$ for all $t$ gives $J_{t_0}(\partial_t J_t|_{t_0}) + (\partial_t J_t|_{t_0}) J_{t_0} = 0$ on differentiating.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Step 2: Family-variation of the cotangent $J_t^*$-action.}\enspace By duality, $J_t^*$ on the cotangent space $T_x^* X_t \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C}$ has $\pm i$-eigenspaces $T_x^{*(1,0)} X_t \oplus T_x^{*(0,1)} X_t$. The infinitesimal variation $\partial_t J_t^*|_{t_0}$ anti-commutes with $J_{t_0}^*$, hence sends $T_x^{*(1,0)} X_{t_0}$ (the $+i$-eigenspace) into $T_x^{*(0,1)} X_{t_0}$ (the $-i$-eigenspace) and vice versa. (The anti-commutation $J_{t_0}^*(\partial_t J_t^*) + (\partial_t J_t^*) J_{t_0}^* = 0$ implies that $\partial_t J_t^*$ exchanges $\pm i$-eigenspaces.)

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Step 3: Family-variation of wedge-power eigenspaces.}\enspace By \emph{[Master-Monograph-Supplement-II]} Theorem 3.2.5.1, $\bigwedge^p T_x^{*(1,0)} X_t \otimes \bigwedge^q T_x^{*(0,1)} X_t$ is the $i^{p - q}$-eigenspace of the wedge-extended $J_t^*$-action. The infinitesimal variation of this eigenspace at $t_0$ is computed by Leibniz: $\partial_t$ acts on a wedge product $\alpha_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \alpha_p \wedge \beta_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \beta_q$ by the sum of terms with one $\alpha_i$ or $\beta_j$ replaced by its $\partial_t$-derivative. By Step 2, $\partial_t \alpha_i$ (for $\alpha_i \in T_x^{*(1,0)} X_{t_0}$) is a $(0,1)$-form at $t_0$, and $\partial_t \beta_j$ (for $\beta_j \in T_x^{*(0,1)} X_{t_0}$) is a $(1,0)$-form at $t_0$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Step 4: The bigrading shift.}\enspace Replacing a $(1, 0)$-factor $\alpha_i$ by its $\partial_t$-derivative (a $(0, 1)$-factor) shifts the wedge-product from $(p, q)$-type to $(p-1, q+1)$-type. Similarly, replacing a $(0, 1)$-factor $\beta_j$ by its $\partial_t$-derivative (a $(1, 0)$-factor) shifts from $(p, q)$ to $(p+1, q-1)$. The first-order variation of a $(p, q)$-form is therefore in $(p-1, q+1) \oplus (p+1, q-1)$-type.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Step 5: Hodge filtration.}\enspace The Hodge filtration $F^p = \bigoplus_{a \geq p} H^{a, n-a}$ on $H^n(X_t, \mathbb{C})$ contains all $(a, b)$-type with $a \geq p$. By Step 4, the first-order variation of an $F^p$-class includes $(p-1, n - p + 1)$-type, which is in $F^{p-1}$. The $(p+1, n - p - 1)$-type component is also in $F^p$, hence \emph{a fortiori} in $F^{p-1}$. Therefore the first-order variation lies in $F^{p-1}$:
$$\partial_t F^p(t)|_{t_0} \;\subset\; F^{p-1}(t_0).$$
This is Griffiths transversality. $\square$

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What Theorem 6.0.3.1 establishes.}\enspace 
The Griffiths transversality condition of Deligne \S 1 is,, the family-variation of the local-cotangent $J^* = \pm i$-eigenspace decomposition of \emph{[Master-Monograph-Supplement-II]} Theorem 3.2.5.1. The Kodaira--Spencer anti-commutation of $\partial_t J_t$ with $J_{t_0}$ is the source of the bigrading shift $(p, q) \to (p-1, q+1) \oplus (p+1, q-1)$ that makes Griffiths transversality hold. The framework's $+i$-orientation content (Master Monograph Definition 3.1, from $dx_4/dt = +ic$) supplies the local holomorphicity that is varied; the family-variation is the standard Kodaira--Spencer deformation theory.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What Theorem 6.0.3.1 does \emph{not} establish.}\enspace 

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(N1)}\enspace The theorem does \emph{not} prove a new transversality result. The Griffiths transversality theorem of Griffiths 1968, 1970 is proven by exactly the Kodaira--Spencer mechanism the present theorem identifies; the present theorem identifies that this mechanism is the family-variation of the framework's $J$-structure.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(N2)}\enspace The theorem does \emph{not} engage the Cattani--Deligne--Kaplan 1995 result (Deligne 2006 ref [4]) that the locus of Hodge classes is a countable union of complex-analytic subvarieties of $T$. The present theorem supplies the framework's reading of the transversality phenomenon; the algebraicity of the Hodge locus is a deeper open question, outside the present scope.
