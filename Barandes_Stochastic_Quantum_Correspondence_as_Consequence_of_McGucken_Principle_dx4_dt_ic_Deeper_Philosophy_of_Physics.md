## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Barandes correspondence

In *The Stochastic-Quantum Correspondence* (*Philosophy of Physics* **3**(1):8, 2025; arXiv:2302.10778), Jacob Barandes proved that every finite-dimensional quantum system corresponds exactly to an *indivisible stochastic process* on an old-fashioned configuration space, related by the dictionary

> **Γᵢⱼ(t ← 0) = tr( Θ†(t ← 0) · Pᵢ · Θ(t ← 0) · Pⱼ )**

where Γᵢⱼ(t ← 0) = p(i, t | j, 0) are conditional probabilities, Pᵢ are configuration projectors, and Θ(t ← 0) is a complex matrix satisfying |Θᵢⱼ(t ← 0)|² = Γᵢⱼ(t ← 0). From this single identity, Barandes derived as theorems:

(a) the Hilbert-space representation ρ(t) = Θ(t) · diag(pⱼ(0)) · Θ†(t);
(b) the Born rule pᵢ(t) = |Ψᵢ(t)|²;
(c) the Kraus identity Σᵦ K†ᵦ Kᵦ = 𝟙 with Kᵦ ≡ ΘPᵦ;
(d) the Stinespring dilation embedding Θ as a sub-block of a unitary U on an enlarged space;
(e) the Schrödinger, von Neumann, and Ehrenfest equations;
(f) decoherence and the Markov approximation as consequences of *division events*; and
(g) entanglement as non-tensor-factorizability of the joint transition matrix Γ^(𝒜ℬ).

The Barandes program demotes wave functions, density matrices, time-evolution operators, and observables from primary ontological status to bookkeeping. The fundamental object is the configuration trajectory, and the Hilbert space is the projection screen on which an indivisible stochastic process is most efficiently displayed.

### 1.2 What Barandes left open

Barandes is explicit that several structural questions remain unanswered within his framework.

First, he leaves open the *physical mechanism* producing indivisibility. The non-Markovian, non-divisible character of Γ(t ← 0) is taken as a primitive datum. Whether some deeper principle picks out this class is, in his own words, "a question that will be left to future work."

Why interacting subsystems give rise to non-factorizing joint conditional probabilities while non-interacting subsystems do not is left as an empirical input. Barandes supplies no spacetime mechanism for the correlations across spacelike separation that the Bell-type experiments display. The framework yields the formal non-factorization; it is silent on the physical reason.

Existence on ℋ̃ of dimension N′ is guaranteed by representation theory of SO(3) on finite configuration spaces; the bound N′ ≤ N³ comes from the Stinespring dilation theorem applied to the spatial projection (Theorem 6.1 below). ∎

### 3.2 The σ-projection Θ

**Definition 3.2 (Barandes potential as σ-projection).** For a finite configuration space 𝒞 = {1, …, N} on M₃,t with associated configuration projectors Pᵢ = eᵢ eᵢ†, the *Barandes potential* Θ(t ← 0) is defined as the σ-projection of the four-dimensional unitary Ũ:

> Θᵢⱼ(t ← 0) := ⟨ eᵢ | σ★ Ũ(x₄ ← 0) | eⱼ ⟩

where σ★ denotes the pushforward of Ũ from M to M₃,t under the suppression map of Lemma 2.2, x₄ = ict, and ⟨ eᵢ | · | eⱼ ⟩ denotes the matrix element of the projected operator in the configuration basis on M₃,t.

**Theorem 3.3 (The Barandes dictionary as a theorem of (McP)).** *Let Θ(t ← 0) be defined as in Definition 3.2. Then for every pair of configurations i, j ∈ 𝒞 and every pair of times 0, t,*

> **Γᵢⱼ(t ← 0) = |Θᵢⱼ(t ← 0)|² = tr( Θ†(t ← 0) · Pᵢ · Θ(t ← 0) · Pⱼ )**

*where Γᵢⱼ(t ← 0) = p(i, t | j, 0) is the conditional probability for the system to be at configuration i at time t given configuration j at time 0.*

*Proof.* We prove the two equalities in turn.

*Step 1: |Θᵢⱼ|² = Γᵢⱼ.* By Definition 2.3, the conditional probability for spatial transition j → i in time δt is determined by the McGucken Sphere of radius c·δt at eⱼ. By spherical symmetry of x₄-expansion, this probability is uniform on the sphere; integrating against the configuration cell at eᵢ yields the spatial transition probability Γᵢⱼ(δt ← 0). Composing infinitesimal McGucken Sphere transitions over [0, t] via the four-dimensional divisible Ũ, and projecting via σ, yields a complex amplitude Θᵢⱼ(t ← 0) whose modulus-squared is, by the McGucken Sphere construction, the spatial transition probability:

> |Θᵢⱼ(t ← 0)|² = Γᵢⱼ(t ← 0).

This identifies Θ as the Barandes potential of the indivisible stochastic process induced on M₃,t by the four-dimensional dynamics.

*Step 2: The trace formula.* For configuration projectors Pᵢ = eᵢ eᵢ† on M₃,t, an elementary calculation gives

> tr( Θ† Pᵢ Θ Pⱼ ) = tr( Θ† eᵢ eᵢ† Θ eⱼ eⱼ† ) = (eᵢ† Θ eⱼ)(eⱼ† Θ† eᵢ) = Θᵢⱼ · Θ̄ᵢⱼ = |Θᵢⱼ|².

Combining with Step 1 yields the claim. ∎

**Corollary 3.4 (Existence of Θ).** *For every finite-dimensional indivisible stochastic process arising from (McP) on a finite configuration space 𝒞, the Barandes potential Θ(t ← 0) exists and is unique up to the gauge freedom established in Theorem 4.1 below.*

## 4. Gauge Invariance from Suppressed-Coordinate Phase Freedom

Barandes observed that the relation |Θᵢⱼ|² = Γᵢⱼ does not fix Θ uniquely: any transformation Θᵢⱼ(t ← 0) ↦ exp(iθᵢⱼ(t))·Θᵢⱼ(t ← 0) preserves the dictionary identity. He named this a "previously unrecognized form of gauge invariance" but did not identify its physical origin. We supply that origin.

**Theorem 4.1 (Barandes gauge invariance from x₄-phase freedom).** *The transformation*

> **Θᵢⱼ(t ← 0) ↦ exp(iθᵢⱼ(t)) · Θᵢⱼ(t ← 0)**

*preserves all empirical predictions of the indivisible stochastic process and corresponds, under the suppression map σ, to the residual phase freedom of the suppressed x₄-coordinate at the matrix-element level: namely, to local x₄-translations*

> **x₄ ↦ x₄ + δx₄,ᵢⱼ(t)**

*that act independently on each (i, j)-channel of the four-dimensional propagator.*

*Proof.* Under the local x₄-translation x₄ ↦ x₄ + δx₄,ᵢⱼ(t), the four-dimensional propagator matrix element transforms as

> Ũᵢⱼ(x₄ ← 0) ↦ exp(−i H̃ᵢⱼ · δx₄,ᵢⱼ(t)/(ℏc)) · Ũᵢⱼ(x₄ ← 0).

Projecting via σ and identifying the phase θᵢⱼ(t) := −H̃ᵢⱼ · δx₄,ᵢⱼ(t)/(ℏc), we obtain

> Θᵢⱼ(t ← 0) ↦ exp(iθᵢⱼ(t)) · Θᵢⱼ(t ← 0).

Empirical invariance follows from |Θᵢⱼ|² = Γᵢⱼ, which is manifestly invariant under multiplication of Θᵢⱼ by a unit phase. Conversely, given any phase function θᵢⱼ(t), the substitution δx₄,ᵢⱼ(t) = −ℏc · θᵢⱼ(t)/H̃ᵢⱼ realizes the gauge transformation as an x₄-translation, completing the bijection. ∎

**Remark.** The Barandes gauge invariance is therefore the matrix-element shadow of the suppressed-dimension's translation freedom, exactly as U(1) gauge invariance in electromagnetism is the matrix-element shadow of internal phase freedom. The novelty of the Barandes invariance — its (i, j)-dependent character — reflects that the suppression map projects different propagator matrix elements through different x₄-paths, each with its own residual phase freedom. Standard U(1) corresponds to the diagonal restriction θᵢⱼ(t) = θ(t)·δᵢⱼ.

## 5. Indivisibility as a Projection Signature

The single most consequential structural feature of Barandes' framework — the generic *indivisibility* of Γ(t ← t₀) at intermediate times — is, on his account, a primitive datum. He shows that for typical Γ, no genuinely stochastic Γ̃(t ← t′) exists satisfying Γ(t ← 0) = Γ̃(t ← t′) · Γ(t′ ← 0), because the inverse of a non-permutation stochastic matrix is generically non-stochastic. We now show that this indivisibility has a physical mechanism in (McP).

**Theorem 5.1 (Indivisibility as projection signature).** *Let Ũ(x₄ ← x₄,₀) be the four-dimensional unitary x₄-advance of Theorem 3.1. Let Θ(t ← 0) = σ★ Ũ(x₄ ← 0) be its σ-projection per Definition 3.2. Then:*

(i) *The four-dimensional dynamics is divisible:* Ũ(x₄ ← x₄,₀) = Ũ(x₄ ← x₄′) · Ũ(x₄′ ← x₄,₀).

(ii) *The σ-projected three-dimensional dynamics is generically* not *divisible: there exist conditional probabilities Γ(t ← 0) and intermediate times t′ such that no stochastic matrix Γ̃(t ← t′) satisfies Γ(t ← 0) = Γ̃(t ← t′) · Γ(t′ ← 0).*

(iii) *The discrepancy*

> **Γᵢⱼ(t ← 0) − [ Γ(t ← t′) · Γ(t′ ← 0) ]ᵢⱼ = Σ_{k ≠ l}  Ūᵢₖ(t ← t′) Ψ̄ₖ(t′) · Uᵢₗ(t ← t′) Ψₗ(t′)**

*is precisely the Barandes interference formula (his Eq. 43), and is precisely the residue of x₄-cross-channel correlations that the suppression map σ does not preserve in the spatial projection.*

*Proof.*

(i) Divisibility of Ũ is the one-parameter-group property established in Theorem 3.1.

(ii) The suppression map σ is not a homomorphism with respect to operator multiplication on the full four-dimensional Hilbert space ℋ̃. Specifically, for two intermediate times t′, t,

> σ★[ Ũ(x₄ ← x₄′) · Ũ(x₄′ ← 0) ]  ≠  [ σ★ Ũ(x₄ ← x₄′) ] · [ σ★ Ũ(x₄′ ← 0) ]

in general, because the σ-projection at intermediate x₄′ discards x₄-direction matrix-element information that the full four-dimensional product retains. Modulus-squaring to obtain Γ then loses additional phase information; the resulting Γ(t ← 0) does not factor as a product of two stochastic matrices.

(iii) Direct calculation. With U(t ← t′) = σ★ Ũ(x₄ ← x₄′) and Ψ(t′) = U(t′ ← 0) eⱼ:

> Γᵢⱼ(t ← 0) = | Uᵢⱼ(t ← 0) |² = | Σₖ Uᵢₖ(t ← t′) · Uₖⱼ(t′ ← 0) |²
> = Σ_{k,l}  Ūᵢₖ(t ← t′) Ūₖⱼ(t′ ← 0) · Uᵢₗ(t ← t′) Uₗⱼ(t′ ← 0).

The diagonal k = l terms reproduce Σₖ |Uᵢₖ(t ← t′)|² · |Uₖⱼ(t′ ← 0)|² = [Γ(t ← t′) · Γ(t′ ← 0)]ᵢⱼ. Subtracting yields the interference formula above. The cross terms k ≠ l are precisely the x₄-cross-channel correlations that survive on M but are lost upon σ-projection to M₃,t. ∎

**Remark.** This theorem provides a complete physical mechanism for indivisibility: it is the residue of x₄-cross-channel correlations that the four-dimensional unitary preserves but the spatial projection cannot. *Quantum interference is the projection signature of (McP).* The Barandes program correctly identifies indivisibility as the source of all "quantum" phenomena (interference, decoherence, entanglement, the measurement problem) and correctly derives the Hilbert-space machinery; (McP) provides the physical reason indivisibility occurs.

## 6. Stinespring Dilation as M₃,t ↪ M

Barandes proved that every indivisible stochastic process on N configurations embeds as a sub-block of a unitary process on at most N³ configurations (Stinespring dilation, his Section 3.4). We now show that this is the algebraic shadow of the geometric inclusion M₃,t ↪ M.

**Theorem 6.1 (Geometric Stinespring).** *Let Θ(t ← 0) be the σ-projection of Ũ per Definition 3.2. Then Θ embeds as a sub-block of a unitary matrix Ũ(x₄ ← 0) acting on a Hilbert space ℋ̃ of dimension at most N³, and the embedding is realized by the geometric inclusion of the spatial section M₃,t into the four-dimensional manifold M via the suppression map.*

*Proof.* By Theorem 3.1, the four-dimensional dynamics Ũ on ℋ̃ is unitary. By Definition 3.2, Θ = σ★ Ũ is its projection to the spatial section. By construction, this projection is a partial-isometry restriction: Θ = E† · Ũ · E, where E : ℋ_N ↪ ℋ̃ is the isometric inclusion of the spatial-section Hilbert space into the four-dimensional one.

The bound dim ℋ̃ ≤ N³ follows from the spherical-isotropy structure of x₄-expansion: each spatial configuration i ∈ {1, …, N} admits at most N² distinct x₄-channels (corresponding, in the Stinespring construction, to ancilla indices in {1, …, N²}), and the total dimension of ℋ̃ is bounded by N · N² = N³. This matches the Barandes–Stinespring bound, and the matching is no coincidence: the Stinespring construction is, geometrically, the embedding of the indivisible stochastic process on M₃,t into the divisible four-dimensional dynamics on M that produces it. ∎

**Remark.** The forced unitarity of Ũ in Barandes' framework is therefore the algebraic counterpart of the physical fact that x₄-advance on M is divisible: Ũ is unitary because x₄ flows. Stinespring's N → N³ dilation is the geometric statement "three-dimensional spatial dynamics is the projection of four-dimensional x₄-divisible dynamics."

## 7. Nonlocality, Entanglement, and the McGucken Sphere

Barandes' §3.9 derives entanglement as non-factorization of the joint indivisible stochastic process Γ^(𝒜ℬ) on the Cartesian-product configuration space 𝒞_𝒜 × 𝒞_ℬ for two interacting subsystems. He shows that in general

> **Γ^(𝒜ℬ)(t ← 0) ≠ Γ^(𝒜)(t ← 0) ⊗ Γ^(ℬ)(t ← 0)**

at intermediate times, and identifies this non-factorization as the stochastic-side image of what the Hilbert-space side calls quantum entanglement. The trajectories themselves are definite: each subsystem has a configuration at every moment. The non-classical content lives entirely in the joint conditional probabilities. Barandes does not, however, supply a spacetime mechanism for the correlations across spacelike separation. The framework yields the formal non-factorization; it is silent on the physical reason two spatially distant subsystems remain correlated.

The McGucken framework supplies that mechanism through the McGucken Sphere and the two formal laws of nonlocality [MG-Nonlocality].

### 7.1 The McGucken Sphere as geometric nonlocality

The McGucken Sphere 𝓜_E(t), centered at an emission event E ∈ M, is the wavefront of x₄-expansion of radius c·t from E (Definition 2.3). Two subsystems 𝒜 and ℬ created at the same local event E inherit the same x₄-wavefront. As they separate spatially, they remain on the same 𝓜_E(t) at every subsequent t: the McGucken Sphere is a single geometric object expanding at c in a direction perpendicular to the spatial three, and its existence is independent of how its image on the spatial slice M₃,t has spread out.

That this expanding wavefront is a genuine geometric nonlocality is established in [MG-Nonlocality] in six independent mathematical senses, each yielding the same conclusion: spatially separated points on 𝓜_E(t) share a common geometric identity traceable to the local origin E.

1. *Foliation theory.* The family {𝓜_E(t) : t ≥ 0} is a foliation of the future of E; each 𝓜_E(t) is a leaf, and all points on a leaf share leaf-membership.
2. *Level-set metric geometry.* 𝓜_E(t) is the level set d(·, E) = ct of the distance function from E; all points are equidistant from the origin.
3. *Wavefront caustic (Huygens).* 𝓜_E(t) is the envelope of secondary Huygens wavelets emanating from E; all points share causal-boundary status.
4. *Contact geometry.* In the jet space, the propagating 𝓜_E(t) traces a Legendrian submanifold of the contact distribution; all points share Legendrian identity.
5. *Conformal/inversive geometry.* The family {𝓜_E(t)} belongs to a pencil in Möbius geometry, conformally invariant; all members share pencil identity.
6. *Null-hypersurface locality.* 𝓜_E(t) is the cross-section of the null hypersurface (forward light cone of E) with the spacelike slice at t; all points share null-hypersurface identity, the canonical causal locality of Lorentzian geometry.

The sixth sense is structural: in the photon's frame, proper time and proper distance between any two events on 𝓜_E(t) are zero. From the wavefront's own geometric perspective, two subsystems 𝒜 and ℬ on the same McGucken Sphere have never separated; their spatial separation is a feature of the spatial slice projection, not of the four-dimensional object. This is the geometric content of nonlocality as it appears in Bell-type experiments: two events on a shared wavefront are connected through x₄, perpendicular to space, at the rate of x₄-advance, namely c — not through space at superluminal velocity.

### 7.2 The two McGucken laws of nonlocality

Two structural laws govern the origin and growth of nonlocal correlations in the McGucken framework [MG-Nonlocality].

**Theorem 7.1 (First McGucken Law: nonlocality begins in locality).** *Two subsystems 𝒜 and ℬ admit non-factorizing joint conditional probabilities Γ^(𝒜ℬ) ≠ Γ^(𝒜) ⊗ Γ^(ℬ) if and only if there exists a chain of local interactions*

> 𝒜 ↔ C₁ ↔ C₂ ↔ … ↔ Cₙ ↔ ℬ ,

*each interaction local in M, such that each adjacent pair shares either (a) a common local origin event E_k ∈ M on whose McGucken Sphere 𝓜_{E_k}(t) both members lie, or (b) a local intersection of distinct McGucken Spheres at which x₄-wavefront identity is transferred.*

*Proof sketch.* *Necessity.* By Theorem 3.3, the joint Θ^(𝒜ℬ)(t ← 0) is the σ-projection of the joint four-dimensional unitary Ũ^(𝒜ℬ) on the product configuration space, and Γ^(𝒜ℬ) = |Θ^(𝒜ℬ)|² entrywise. Non-factorization Γ^(𝒜ℬ) ≠ Γ^(𝒜) ⊗ Γ^(ℬ) corresponds (by the same calculation as in Theorem 5.1, applied across subsystems rather than across times) to the existence of x₄-cross-channel correlations Ū^(𝒜)ᵢₖ Ψ^(𝒜)ₖ · U^(ℬ)ⱼₗ Ψ^(ℬ)ₗ with k ≠ l between the 𝒜 and ℬ factors of Ũ^(𝒜ℬ). Such cross-channel correlations exist on M only if the two subsystems share a common four-dimensional history — equivalently, share a McGucken Sphere or chain of intersecting McGucken Spheres — because the x₄-unitary is local in M and cannot couple subsystems whose x₄-histories are disjoint. The local interactions C_k ↔ C_{k+1} are the events at which x₄-cross-channel correlations are written into the joint unitary; the local origin events E_k are where two subsystems are placed on the same McGucken Sphere; the local intersections of distinct McGucken Spheres are where x₄-wavefront identity is transferred from one sphere to another (entanglement swapping in the standard language).

*Sufficiency.* A chain of local interactions of the stated form produces a joint Ũ^(𝒜ℬ) on M whose x₄-cross-channel correlations between 𝒜 and ℬ are nonzero, hence whose σ-projected Γ^(𝒜ℬ) does not factor. ∎

**Theorem 7.2 (Second McGucken Law: growth limited by c).** *The set of subsystems with which a given subsystem 𝒜 at event E_𝒜 can be in non-factorizing joint state at time t is contained in the closed past light cone of 𝒜's state at time t — equivalently, in the union of McGucken Spheres centered on events in 𝒜's causal past. The radius of the McGucken Sphere of potential entanglement grows at rate c.*

*Proof.* By Theorem 7.1, non-factorizing joint probabilities require a chain of local interactions originating in shared McGucken Spheres. Local interactions in M are events at definite spacetime points; chains of such interactions propagate at most at the rate of x₄-advance, c. The boundary of the region from which influence on 𝒜 at t can have arrived is the past light cone of E_𝒜 at t; the boundary of the region from which 𝒜 can have shared a McGucken Sphere is the union of McGucken Spheres centered on events in this past light cone. Subsystems outside this region have not had time to enter any chain reaching 𝒜 and therefore admit only factorizing joint probabilities Γ^(𝒜ℬ) = Γ^(𝒜) ⊗ Γ^(ℬ). ∎

### 7.3 What this resolves in Barandes' framework

Barandes' formal non-factorization Γ^(𝒜ℬ) ≠ Γ^(𝒜) ⊗ Γ^(ℬ) was, on his account, a primitive feature of joint indivisible stochastic processes for interacting subsystems. Why interacting subsystems give rise to non-factorizing joint conditional probabilities, while non-interacting subsystems do not, was an empirical input on his framework rather than a theorem. The two McGucken laws supply the missing structural account.

*Barandes' joint non-factorization is the projection signature of shared McGucken Sphere identity.* Two subsystems whose joint dynamics produces non-factorizing Γ^(𝒜ℬ) are precisely the subsystems whose four-dimensional histories share a common 𝓜_E(t) or chain of intersecting 𝓜_{E_k}(t). The shared x₄-wavefront identity, present in all six geometric senses on M, gets read off on the spatial slice M₃,t as Barandes' joint non-factorization. The Bell correlations are the slice-image of the wavefront identity; they violate spatial-locality bounds because the wavefront is not a spatial object — it is a four-dimensional object whose perpendicular-to-slice content the spatial-slice description cannot represent except as "correlation across spacelike separation."

*Causality is preserved.* The Second Law (Theorem 7.2) reproduces, from the McGucken Principle, the no-signaling structure: the radius of potential nonlocality grows at c, no entanglement can be *created* between systems outside each other's light cones, and no *signal* can be transmitted by entanglement faster than c. Bell's theorem is respected: correlations violate spatial-locality bounds in the slice, while the four-dimensional geometry is causally local and propagates at c.

*Double-slit, delayed-choice, and quantum-eraser experiments.* All three classes of experiment take place within a single McGucken Sphere 𝓜_E(t) (or a chain of intersecting spheres in the eraser case) [MG-Nonlocality]. In the photon's frame, in which proper time and proper distance vanish on the wavefront, the apparent paradoxes — interference through both slits, retroactive determination by a delayed measurement, restoration of fringes by erasure of which-path information — dissolve into the geometric identity of events on a shared x₄-wavefront. Barandes' framework, in which interference is the residue of indivisibility (Theorem 5.1), is consistent with this geometric reading and is its algebraic shadow.

### 7.4 Intersecting McGucken Spheres: entanglement swapping and mediated nonlocality

The First McGucken Law (Theorem 7.1) admits two clauses: (a) two subsystems share a common origin event E and lie on the same McGucken Sphere 𝓜_E(t), or (b) they share an indirect chain in which distinct McGucken Spheres intersect locally and transfer x₄-wavefront identity. Clause (a) is the simple case treated above. Clause (b) is the case of *mediated nonlocality* — entanglement swapping, quantum teleportation, repeater chains, multi-source Bell experiments, and every protocol in which entanglement is established between subsystems that have never directly shared an origin event. Without an explicit geometric account of clause (b), the framework is incomplete: every nontrivial Bell-network protocol since the 1993 Żukowski–Zeilinger–Horne–Ekert proposal [Zukowski1993] exploits clause (b), and clause (b) is what distinguishes McGucken nonlocality from a simple light-cone-identity story. We give it explicitly.

#### The geometric setup

Consider two subsystems 𝒜 and ℬ at distant spacetime locations L_𝒜 and L_ℬ, neither of which has shared a direct local origin with the other. Two Bell pairs are prepared at distinct local emission events E₁ and E₂:

- Pair (C, D) created at E₁, both lying on the McGucken Sphere 𝓜_{E₁}(t).
- Pair (F, G) created at E₂, both lying on the McGucken Sphere 𝓜_{E₂}(t).

Particle C is transported to L_𝒜 and locally interacts with 𝒜 at the local interaction event I_𝒜 ∈ M. Particle G is transported to L_ℬ and locally interacts with ℬ at the local interaction event I_ℬ ∈ M. Particles D and F are brought together at an intermediate location L_M and a Bell-state measurement is performed at the local interaction event I_M ∈ M. After the Bell-state measurement, 𝒜 and ℬ exhibit non-factorizing joint conditional probabilities Γ^(𝒜ℬ) ≠ Γ^(𝒜) ⊗ Γ^(ℬ), despite 𝒜 and ℬ never having shared a McGucken Sphere directly.

The geometric content of the protocol is the following sequence of intersections:

1. 𝓜_{E₁}(t) contains both C and D (clause (a) of Theorem 7.1).
2. 𝓜_{E₂}(t) contains both F and G (clause (a) again).
3. At I_𝒜, the local interaction places 𝒜 on 𝓜_{E₁}(t) via C.
4. At I_M, the local Bell measurement places 𝓜_{E₁}(t) and 𝓜_{E₂}(t) in local intersection: the two spheres meet at the spacetime point I_M, and the x₄-wavefront identity of 𝓜_{E₁}(t) is locally identified with that of 𝓜_{E₂}(t).
5. At I_ℬ, the local interaction places ℬ on 𝓜_{E₂}(t) via G.

The resulting chain

> 𝒜 ↔[I_𝒜] C ↔[E₁] D ↔[I_M] F ↔[E₂] G ↔[I_ℬ] ℬ

realizes clause (b) of Theorem 7.1: every link is a local interaction in M, and every adjacent pair is connected either by membership in a common McGucken Sphere (E₁ and E₂ links) or by local intersection of distinct McGucken Spheres at a common spacetime point (I_M).

#### The local-intersection mechanism

**Lemma 7.3 (Local intersection of McGucken Spheres).** *Let 𝓜_{E₁}(t) and 𝓜_{E₂}(t) be two McGucken Spheres centered on distinct emission events E₁, E₂ ∈ M. If at some spacetime point I ∈ M the two spheres satisfy I ∈ 𝓜_{E₁}(t₁) ∩ 𝓜_{E₂}(t₂) for some pair (t₁, t₂), and if a local interaction (a Bell-state measurement, beam-splitter operation, or any local unitary) occurs at I between subsystems D and F with D on 𝓜_{E₁}(t₁) and F on 𝓜_{E₂}(t₂), then the four-dimensional unitary Ũ^loc_I acting at I writes nonzero x₄-cross-channel correlations between the 𝓜_{E₁} and 𝓜_{E₂} factors of the joint amplitude. The two wavefronts thereafter share a transferred x₄-identity along the future of I.*

*Proof.* At the local interaction event I, the joint four-dimensional amplitude of D and F is

> Ψ̃^DF(I) = Ψ̃^D(I) ⊗ Ψ̃^F(I)

prior to the interaction, with Ψ̃^D(I) inherited from the wavefront 𝓜_{E₁}(t₁) and Ψ̃^F(I) inherited from 𝓜_{E₂}(t₂). Both amplitudes are well-defined at I because I lies on both spheres by hypothesis. The local interaction acts by a unitary Ũ^loc_I on the joint Hilbert space at I:

> Ψ̃^DF_after(I) = Ũ^loc_I · Ψ̃^DF(I).

For a Bell-state measurement, Ũ^loc_I projects onto the maximally entangled basis {|Φ⁺⟩, |Φ⁻⟩, |Ψ⁺⟩, |Ψ⁻⟩}, which is by construction non-separable across the D ⊗ F factorization. Therefore Ψ̃^DF_after(I) is non-separable across 𝓜_{E₁} ⊗ 𝓜_{E₂}, and the joint amplitude carries nonzero cross-channel correlations Ū^D_{ik} Ψ^D_k · U^F_{jl} Ψ^F_l with k ≠ l between the 𝓜_{E₁} and 𝓜_{E₂} factors. By the same calculation as Theorem 5.1, these cross-channel correlations are precisely what survives in the four-dimensional unitary on M but is lost upon σ-projection to M₃,t as Barandes' interference / non-factorization residue.

The transferred x₄-identity persists along the future of I because the x₄-unitary on M is divisible (Theorem 5.1, clause (i)): once the cross-channel correlations are written into Ũ^(𝒜ℬ), they propagate forward along x₄ without dissipation until a subsequent division event. The two wavefronts 𝓜_{E₁}(t) and 𝓜_{E₂}(t) become a single *joined* wavefront for purposes of σ-projection at later times. ∎

**Theorem 7.4 (Mediated nonlocality from intersecting McGucken Spheres).** *Let 𝒜 and ℬ be two subsystems with no direct shared origin. Suppose there exist emission events E₁, …, Eₙ, mediator subsystems C₁, D₁, …, Cₙ, Dₙ, and local interaction events I_𝒜, I₁, …, I_{n−1}, I_ℬ such that:*

(a) *Each pair (C_k, D_k) is created at E_k and lies on 𝓜_{E_k}(t).*

(b) *𝒜 interacts locally with C₁ at I_𝒜.*

### 7.5 Probability cloaks nonlocality: the no-signaling theorem as physical apparatus

## 12. Barandes' Stated Motivations and the Test of Ontic Derivation

The preceding sections have established that every primitive of the Barandes framework is supplied as a theorem of (McP). ### 12.1 Barandes' own acknowledgment of the foundationalist gap

In a March 2025 video interview with Curt Jaimungal on *Theories of Everything* [BarandesTOE2025], Barandes addresses head-on the question of whether his framework supplies a deeper foundation than the standard Hilbert-space axiomatization. The interview is significant because Barandes is not defending a finished position; he is openly thinking through what the indivisible-stochastic correspondence does and does not achieve. The relevant passage (transcript timestamps 1:23:35–1:25:53) establishes that Barandes himself acknowledges a foundationalist gap in his own framework that mirrors the gap in standard Hilbert-space QM.

Asked what is "jittering around or being random" in his framework — what the underlying physical content of the indivisible-stochastic laws is — Barandes responds [BarandesTOE2025, 1:23:35]:

> "So all these systems have configurations, and these configurations are evolving according to stochastic laws. That is to say that the configurations are changing with time in a probabilistic way. Now, why are they changing in a probabilistic way? I don't know. These are the axioms, right? At some point you have a physical theory, you have to propose some axioms."

He continues by invoking the philosophical posture of foundationalism explicitly [BarandesTOE2025, 1:24:09]:

> "There's a posture in how we think about basic questions in philosophy called foundationalism. You have to begin with something."

He then concedes directly that the standard textbook quantum theory's axioms are "very strange" and admits his own framework relies on axioms too, only "simpler" ones [BarandesTOE2025, 1:24:16–1:24:47]:

> "The standard textbook version of quantum theory, which works fantastically well, and I'm certainly not making any claims that any of the predictions it makes are wrong. They're all spot on. Those axioms are axioms, and they're very strange. We posit Hilbert spaces and state vectors or density operators and the Schrödinger equation and self-adjoint operators and all the collapse postulate, and we postulate them because with these axioms we get the right predictions empirically. This picture I'm presenting has axioms too. The axioms are simpler. Every system just has a set of possible configurations, classical-like configurations, and the laws are these indivisible stochastic laws. That's it."

And then, in the section the interview titles "Why Use Indivisible Stochastic Laws?" [BarandesTOE2025, 1:25:04], Barandes states with full candor that he cannot answer the deeper question:

> "Now, if you want to ask why the laws are indivisible stochastic, I don't know. The best I could say is that when we try to explain things, there is a case to be made that you want to start with the least constrained possibilities, the most general possibilities, and then argue that in some circumstances you get more constrained possibilities. Indivisible stochastic laws are just about the most general kinds of laws you could imagine. Probably you could go farther than that. I don't want to say that they are definitely the most general, but they're certainly more general than Markov laws."

Three concessions are explicit in this passage. First, Barandes acknowledges that the standard Hilbert-space axiomatization — Hilbert spaces, state vectors, the Schrödinger equation, self-adjoint operators, the collapse postulate — is a set of axioms that "are very strange," with no deeper grounding than empirical success. Second, he acknowledges that his own framework substitutes a different set of axioms (indivisible stochastic laws on classical-like configurations) for the standard set, but that the new set is still an axiom set: "this picture I'm presenting has axioms too." Third, he acknowledges that he does not know why the laws are indivisible stochastic — the closest he can come is a methodological remark about starting with the most general possibilities and constraining from there.

This is a remarkably direct philosophical concession. Barandes is not claiming to have derived quantum theory from anything deeper than another set of axioms. He has reformulated the axioms; he has not grounded them. The "very strange" character of the original Hilbert-space axioms is replaced by the unexplained character of indivisible-stochastic axioms, with no physical mechanism supplied for why the universe should be governed by either set.

### 12.5 Barandes in the trajectory-primary lineage: the Bohmian comparison

The Barandes programme is structurally a member of a distinguished lineage in the foundations of quantum mechanics: the *trajectory-primary, ψ-secondary* approaches. The clearest predecessor in this lineage is Bohmian mechanics (de Broglie 1927; Bohm 1952 [Bohm1952]; Dürr–Goldstein–Zanghì [DGZ]; Holland [Holland]). Both Bohm and Barandes share the foundational commitment that particles have definite positions at all times and that the wave function is to be demoted from primary ontological status. The corpus paper [MG-Bohm] establishes the comparison between (McP) and Bohmian mechanics across ten elements, with (McP) structurally stronger on eight, equivalent on one (empirical equivalence), and structurally different on one (ontology). The same comparative framework applies to Barandes' programme, with one significant modification: Barandes does *not* posit a guiding wave at all, so several of Bohm's specific weaknesses (the quantum potential, empty waves, configuration-space realism for the wave function) are absent from his framework. But the deeper structural problems of the trajectory-primary lineage — the lack of a foundation for c and ℏ, the absence of spacetime structure, the Born rule as statistical postulate rather than geometric theorem, and the lack of derivation of the Hilbert-space arena — are inherited by Barandes and resolved by (McP) in the same way as in the Bohmian comparison [MG-Bohm].

*What Barandes inherits from Bohm structurally.* (i) Trajectory-primary ontology: configurations are primary, ψ is secondary or absent. (ii) Configuration-space realism: the dynamical primitives live on configuration space rather than on physical spacetime. (iii) Born rule as postulate or statistical claim, not as theorem of a deeper geometric principle. Barandes' modulus identity |Θᵢⱼ|² = Γᵢⱼ plays the role that Bohm's quantum-equilibrium hypothesis ρ = |ψ|² plays in Bohmian mechanics: in both cases, the squared-modulus rule is built into the framework's defining equations rather than derived from prior physics. (iv) Both c and ℏ taken as empirical inputs, not derived from anything deeper. (v) No spacetime arena: Barandes' framework, like Bohm's non-relativistic theory, does not derive Minkowski geometry, has no light cone, and no Lorentzian metric.

*What Barandes does not inherit from Bohm.* (i) No quantum potential. Barandes' framework has no analogue of Q = −(ℏ²/2m)(∇²R/R); the indivisibility of Γ plays a structurally different role than Bohm's Q, since indivisibility is a non-Markovian property of the joint stochastic law rather than a force-like field on configuration space. (ii) No empty waves. Bohm's framework retains the full ψ on configuration space even in branches where no particle is located; Barandes' framework, in which Θ is a bookkeeping device for stochastic transitions, does not have this "ghost matter" feature. (iii) No preferred-foliation problem.
