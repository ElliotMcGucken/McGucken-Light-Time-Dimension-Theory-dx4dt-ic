## §1. Introduction: Constructor Theory and the McGucken Programme

### §1.1 What constructor theory claims to be

Constructor theory was proposed by David Deutsch [Deutsch2013] and developed by Deutsch with Chiara Marletto and a growing circle of collaborators since 2012. The central methodological claim is a *reformulation* of how the laws of physics are expressed. In the conventional formulation — Newtonian, Lagrangian, Hamiltonian, or quantum-mechanical — one writes down a *dynamical law* and a set of *initial conditions*, and the laws then predict the trajectories of the system through configuration space or Hilbert space. Constructor theory inverts this. Rather than "given initial conditions, predict trajectories," it asks: *which physical transformations admit a constructor capable of performing them reliably, repeatedly, and with arbitrarily high accuracy under the laws of physics, and which do not?*

The constructor-theoretic primitives are [Deutsch2013, §1; DeutschMarletto2015, §2]:

- A **substrate** is a physical system with a set of distinguishable attributes.
- An **attribute** is a property a substrate can have or fail to have.
- A **task** is an ordered pair (x, y) of an input attribute x and an output attribute y of a substrate.
- A **constructor** is a physical system that, when supplied with a substrate carrying attribute x, causes the substrate to acquire attribute y, while the constructor itself remains capable of performing the task again (an additional output signal indicates task completion).
- A task is **possible** under the laws of physics if a constructor for it can be built with arbitrarily high accuracy and reliability; **impossible** otherwise.

The laws of physics are then reformulated as statements about which tasks are possible and which are impossible. The "composition principle" of [Deutsch2013, §1.2] asserts that every regular network of possible tasks is itself a possible task; the "interoperability principle" of [DeutschMarletto2015, §2.6] asserts that the combination of two information media is itself an information medium; the "principle of locality" of [DeutschMarletto2015, §2.4] requires that attributes of composite substrates A⊕B factor as pairs (a, b) of attributes of A and B with no global constraint coupling a to b under purely local task performances.

The programme has produced constructor-theoretic treatments of:

1. **Information** [DeutschMarletto2015]. Classical information is the substrate on which copying tasks are possible; quantum information ("superinformation") is the substrate on which copying-all-attributes-simultaneously is impossible while reversible permutation remains possible. The no-cloning theorem, complementarity, and locally inaccessible information in entangled systems are recovered as consequences.

2. **Thermodynamics** [Marletto2016]. The zeroth, first, and second laws are reformulated via constructor-theoretic *adiabatic accessibility*. A "work medium" is a substrate whose energy-storage variable generalizes the weight in a gravitational field of the standard Carathéodory definition. The second law states that certain adiabatic transformations are *not possible* — no constructor for the inverse exists. The first law is the *interoperability* of work media (all ways of doing work on a substrate agree on net energy transfer). An information-theoretic connection emerges on the first law, not merely the second.

3. **Life** [Marletto2014Life]. The Marletto 2014 *Constructor Theory of Life* shows that self-reproduction, replication, and natural selection are possible under "no-design laws" (laws that do not encode the design of biological adaptations) on a single non-trivial condition: that digital information is physically instantiable. The self-reproducer has a structural decomposition into a "replicator" R (storing the recipe) and a "vehicle" V (containing a copier C and a programmable constructor B), with C copying R and B executing the recipe to build a vehicle from generic resources.

4. **Quantum gravity (BMV witness)** [BoseEtAl2017; MarlettoVedral2017Witness]. The Bose–Marletto–Vedral proposal aims to detect quantum gravity at table-top scales by witnessing gravitational entanglement between two masses prepared in spatial superpositions. The constructor-theoretic argument [MarlettoVedral2017Witness] establishes that if a *local* mediator can generate entanglement between two quantum systems, then the mediator itself must be non-classical — making the experiment a witness of non-classicality without assuming any particular formalism for the gravitational field.
