## 2. Where the Two Statements Come From

The McGucken Principle asserts that the fourth dimension x₄ is expanding at the velocity of light: dx₄/dt = ic, with x₄ = ict, as a spherically symmetric McGucken Sphere whose wavelength sets the action h and whose volume defines a nonlocality.

### 2.1 "There is no such thing as now"

The word "now" has two meanings, and they must be kept apart.

**The universal now.** The universal now is one moment shared by the whole universe: all the events, everywhere, that are happening at this instant. Einstein showed in 1905 that two observers in relative motion disagree about which distant events happen at the same time, and that neither observer is wrong. He concluded that "we cannot attach any absolute signification to the concept of simultaneity" [1]. So relativity has no universal now.

**The local now.** The local now is the present moment at one place: the one event in the history of a clock that is happening. Relativity describes the whole history of the clock as a line in spacetime, called its worldline. Every event on the worldline appears in the description on equal terms. No equation of relativity marks one event on the worldline as the present event, and no equation of relativity moves a mark from one event to the next. Hermann Weyl stated the consequence: "The objective world simply is, it does not happen" [2, p. 116]. Rudolf Carnap reported of Einstein that "the problem of the Now worried him seriously," and that Einstein held that the difference between the present and the past and future cannot occur within physics [3, p. 37]. Kurt Gödel wrote that a real passage of time requires layers of "now" that come into existence one after another, and that in relativity "each observer has his own set of 'nows,'" with no set preferred [4, pp. 202–203].

So relativity, taken as the complete description of the world, has no universal now and no local now.

### 2.2 "Entropy always increases"

Rudolf Clausius stated the second law in 1865: "Die Entropie der Welt strebt einem Maximum zu" — the entropy of the world strives toward a maximum [5]. Ludwig Boltzmann showed in 1877 that the entropy of a state is proportional to the logarithm of the number of microscopic arrangements that give that state [6]. In the notation Planck later gave it, S = k log W. Max Planck showed that all observers in relative motion assign a body the same entropy: "the entropy of the body does not depend on the choice of the reference frame" [7]. Arthur Eddington wrote in 1928 that the law that entropy always increases "holds, I think, the supreme position among the laws of Nature" [8, p. 74]. Einstein wrote that classical thermodynamics is the one universal physical theory of which he was convinced that "it will never be overthrown" within the range of its basic concepts [9, p. 33].

The second law is a statement about change. Every use of the law has the same form: a system has some entropy now, and it will have the same or greater entropy later.

## 3. The Paradox Stated Formally

The McGucken Principle asserts that the fourth dimension x₄ is expanding at the velocity of light: dx₄/dt = ic, with x₄ = ict, as a spherically symmetric McGucken Sphere whose wavelength sets the action h and whose volume defines a nonlocality.

### 3.1 Definitions

**Definition (universal now).** A universal now is a set of events, one at every place in the universe, that all observers agree are happening together.

**Definition (local now).** A local now is the one event on a worldline that is present.

**Definition (advance of a now).** A now advances when the present event is replaced by a later event, and that later event is then the present event.

**Definition (increase).** A quantity increases when it has one value at the present event and a greater value after the now has advanced.

By these definitions an increase requires a now that advances.

### 3.2 The three premises

**The No-Now Premise.** Spacetime and the matter and fields in it are the complete description of the world, and in that description there is no universal now and no local now.

**The Second Law Premise.** Entropy always increases.

**The Later-Than-Now Premise.** Entropy will be higher later than it is now.

### 3.3 The contradiction

**Theorem 1 (the McGucken "Entropy Always Increases" and "There Is No Such Thing as Now" Paradox).** The No-Now Premise, the Second Law Premise, and the Later-Than-Now Premise cannot all be true.

*Proof.* Suppose the No-Now Premise is true.

The Later-Than-Now Premise compares the entropy at the present event with the entropy at a later event. By the No-Now Premise there is no present event. The comparison has no first term, so the Later-Than-Now Premise cannot be true.

The Second Law Premise says that entropy increases. By the definition of increase, an increase requires a now that advances. By the No-Now Premise there is no now, so nothing advances. The Second Law Premise cannot be true.

So if the No-Now Premise is true, the other two premises cannot be true. ∎

## 4. What Relativity Alone Keeps and What It Loses

The McGucken Principle asserts that the fourth dimension x₄ is expanding at the velocity of light: dx₄/dt = ic, with x₄ = ict, as a spherically symmetric McGucken Sphere whose wavelength sets the action h and whose volume defines a nonlocality.

### 4.1 Relativity keeps an ordering

Relativistic thermodynamics writes the second law with an entropy current s^μ and the condition ∂_μ s^μ ≥ 0 [10, eq. 2.18]. In words: entropy is created at events and is never destroyed at events. The total entropy on a slice Σ of spacetime is S[Σ] = ∫_Σ s^μ dΣ_μ [10, eq. 2.17].

**Theorem 2 (the ordering).** If ∂_μ s^μ ≥ 0 and no entropy flows in or out at spatial infinity, then for any two slices of spacetime, one lying wholly later than the other, the total entropy on the later slice is equal to or greater than the total entropy on the earlier slice.

*Proof.* Apply Gauss's theorem to the region of spacetime between the two slices. The difference between the two totals equals the integral of ∂_μ s^μ over the region, which is zero or positive. ∎

Theorem 2 holds for every way of slicing spacetime. It needs no universal now.

### 4.2 Relativity loses the increase

**Theorem 3 (no increase).** Under the No-Now Premise, Theorem 2 states an ordering of entropy values and states no increase of entropy.

*Proof.* Under the No-Now Premise the description of the world contains every slice of spacetime and the entropy on every slice, all on equal terms. No slice is the present slice. By the definition of increase, an increase requires a present value that is replaced by a greater value. No value is present, so no value is replaced. What Theorem 2 states is that the entropy on one slice is greater than the entropy on another slice. ∎

Eddington came close to this result in 1928. He held that time's arrow is a property of entropy alone [8, p. 80], and he then found that the association of entropy with "becoming" met obstacles that grew on closer examination [8, p. 96].

### 4.3 Relativity loses the direction

**Theorem 4 (no direction).** Under the No-Now Premise, "entropy always increases" and "entropy always decreases" describe the same spacetime.

*Proof.* The equations of relativity do not say which end of time is the later end. Either end may be named "later," and the spacetime is the same spacetime under either naming. Under one naming the entropy values rise toward the later end. Under the other naming the same entropy values fall toward the later end. Under the No-Now Premise nothing advances toward either end, so nothing in the description selects one naming. ∎

### 4.4 Einstein's two convictions

Einstein held two convictions. He held that thermodynamics "will never be overthrown" [9]. He held that the Now cannot occur within physics [3]. Theorem 1 shows that these two convictions conflict. If the Now is outside physics, then by Theorem 3 the increase of entropy is outside physics as well, and what remains of the second law is the ordering of Theorem 2. Weyl accepted that conclusion [2]. Carnap reports that Einstein accepted the absence of the Now from physics with regret [3].

### 4.5 What a resolution must supply

The second law can be kept as a law of increase only if the No-Now Premise is given up in part. A resolution must supply three things.

**A local now that advances.** There must be a present at every event, and the present must move forward. Theorem 3 requires this.

**No universal now.** Einstein's 1905 result must remain true. Observers in relative motion must still disagree about which distant events happen together.

**A direction of time.** One end of time must be marked by the physics as the later end. Theorem 4 requires this.

### 5.2 A local now that advances

The McGucken Principle dx₄/dt = ic is a statement of a rate. At every point of spacetime, x₄ is expanding. An expansion is a change: the extent of x₄ at a point is greater after than before. The McGucken Principle dx₄/dt = ic therefore places in physics, at every point, something that moves.

The local now at a point is the present extent of the expansion of x₄ at that point. It advances at the rate c. This supplies a local now that advances.

Under the McGucken Principle dx₄/dt = ic the expansion of x₄ proceeds from every point, and every point is the centre of its own McGucken Sphere. The rate c is the same for all observers. The Principle selects no set of events across the universe as the events happening together. Observers in relative motion still disagree about which distant events are simultaneous, as Einstein showed in 1905 [1].

In the McGucken dx₄/dt = ic framework the accumulated x₄-advance along a worldline is X₄ = ic·τ, which is the proper time τ of the worldline multiplied by ic. The two clocks accumulate different x₄-advances. The local nows of different clocks therefore cannot be joined into one universal now. Einstein's 1905 result stands.

Under the McGucken Principle dx₄/dt = ic, "entropy always increases" and "entropy always decreases" are different statements, and the first one is the true one.
