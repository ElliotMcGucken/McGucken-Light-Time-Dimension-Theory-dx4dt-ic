<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Abstract</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The conservation laws are shown to derive from the novel, foundational McGucken Principle which states that the fourth dimension is expanding at the rate of c in a spherically symmetric manner as seen in dx₄/dt = ic. The derivations in this paper are novel and unique as they lead with a geometrical principle and show the conservation laws to be theorems. The McGucken Principle is making waves across diverse realms of physics: [1–7, MG-Proof, MG-Mech, MG-HLA, MG-Born, MG-Born2, MG-Commut, MG-PathInt, MG-Uncertain, MG-Dirac, MG-SecondQ, MG-QED, MG-Maxwell, MG-Wick, MG-SM, MG-Broken, MG-Cabibbo, MG-CKM, MG-Compton, MG-PhotonEntropy, MG-Entropy, MG-Arrows, MG-Nonloc, MG-Nonloc2, MG-Equiv, MG-McGB, MG-Sphere, MG-Sphere2, MG-Bekenstein, MG-Hawking, MG-Susskind, MG-AdSCFT, MG-Verlinde, MG-Jacobson, MG-DarkMatter, MG-Lambda, MG-CosHolo, MG-Horizon, MG-Eleven, MG-Sakharov, MG-Postulates, MG-Constants, MG-Twistor, MG-Woit, MG-String, MG-KK, MG-CMB, MG-Invariance]. From the McGucken Principle, the Minkowski metric, the master equation u^μu_μ = −c², time dilation, length contraction, mass–energy equivalence, the Lorentz transformation, the wave equation, Huygens&#8217; Principle, the Principle of Least Action, the Schrödinger equation, the Klein–Gordon equation, the Dirac equation, the Feynman path integral, the Born rule, the canonical commutation relation [q, p] = iℏ, the Maxwell equations, the Yang–Mills Lagrangian, and the Einstein–Hilbert action follow as theorems [1–15]. The present paper derives, from the same postulate, the complete Noether catalog of continuous symmetries and conservation laws — including energy, momentum, angular momentum, and the boost charges of the Poincaré group; electric charge conservation of U(1); weak isospin conservation of SU(2)_L; color conservation of SU(3)_c; and the covariant energy–momentum conservation ∇_μT^{μν} = 0 of diffeomorphism invariance — and all the accompanying gauge structure, from the single geometric postulate dx₄/dt = ic.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The standard presentation of Noether&#8217;s theorem treats the symmetries of the action as empirical inputs. The present paper derives the symmetries. Temporal uniformity of x₄&#8217;s advance is the time-translation invariance of the action. Spatial homogeneity of x₄&#8217;s expansion is translation invariance. Spherical isotropy of x₄&#8217;s expansion from every event is rotational invariance. Lorentz covariance of x₄&#8217;s rate is Lorentz invariance. The absence of a preferred phase origin on x₄ is global U(1) phase invariance. The gauge field A_μ is the connection on the x₄-orientation bundle; its curvature is F_μν; Maxwell&#8217;s equations follow as the Bianchi identity plus the Euler–Lagrange equations of the unique gauge-invariant kinetic term. The non-Abelian SU(2)_L and SU(3)_c gauge structures are the Clifford-algebraic extensions of the same construction to the transverse-and-spatial rotation sectors of the four-dimensional geometry. Diffeomorphism invariance is the coordinate-independence of the four-dimensional manifold on which x₄ expands.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Every continuous symmetry in the Standard Model and general relativity is a geometric feature of x₄&#8217;s expansion. The full Noether catalog follows as theorems: Poincaré (energy, three momenta, three angular momenta, three boost charges K^i = tP^i − x^iE), internal U(1) (electric charge), non-Abelian SU(2) (weak isospin), non-Abelian SU(3) (color), and diffeomorphism invariance (∇_μT^{μν} = 0). Every conservation law in physics is a shadow of x₄&#8217;s expansion.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The complexification of quantum theory is likewise derived. <h2 class="wp-block-heading">IV. Energy and Momentum Conservation from the Translation Symmetries of x₄&#8217;s Expansion</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This section derives the first four conservation laws of the Poincaré catalog — energy conservation (time-translation) and three-momentum conservation (spatial translations) — from the translation symmetries of x₄&#8217;s expansion. Each derivation follows the chain stated in §II.8: Postulate 1 → geometric symmetry of x₄&#8217;s advance → symmetry of the action → Noether&#8217;s theorem → conservation law.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">IV.1 Energy Conservation: The Standard Derivation</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The standard derivation of energy conservation from Noether&#8217;s theorem takes time-translation invariance of the action as input. If L = L(q, q̇) has no explicit dependence on t, then under the infinitesimal transformation t → t + ε, δL = 0 and the Noether current (III.1) reduces to the conserved Hamiltonian</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">H = p_i q̇^i − L, dH/dt = 0, (IV.1)</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">which is the energy [24].</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The derivation is mathematically transparent. What it does not supply is an account of why the action has no explicit time dependence. In the standard presentation, this is taken as an empirical fact about the laws of physics: experiments performed today produce the same results as experiments performed yesterday, so the Lagrangian must be time-translation invariant. The McGucken framework supplies the geometric origin.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">IV.2 The Geometric Antecedent: Temporal Uniformity of x₄&#8217;s Advance</h3>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading">Proposition IV.1 (Temporal uniformity of x₄&#8217;s advance as time-translation invariance)</h4>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Under the McGucken Principle, the following are equivalent:</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>(a) The free-particle action (II.8) is invariant under the infinitesimal time-translation t → t + ε for every ε ∈ ℝ;</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>(b) The rate dx₄/dt = ic is independent of t: the advance of x₄ proceeds at the same rate ic at every moment.</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Proof.</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>(b) ⇒ (a).</em> By Proposition II.10, S[γ] = −mc ∫_γ |dx₄|. Under t → t + ε, the worldline γ is translated in time but its integrated length along x₄ is unchanged, provided the measure |dx₄| is the same at time t + ε as at time t. By (b), dx₄/dt = ic independent of t, so |dx₄| is the same at every moment, and the integral is invariant. Therefore δS = 0 under t → t + ε.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>(a) ⇒ (b).</em> The free-particle action integrates |dx₄| along γ. If the rate dx₄/dt depended explicitly on t — say, dx₄/dt = if(t)c for some f(t) ≠ 1 — then S would acquire an explicit time dependence through the measure, and δS under t → t + ε would not vanish. Invariance of S for arbitrary γ forces the rate to be t-independent: dx₄/dt = ic at every moment. ∎</p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading">Proposition IV.2 (Energy conservation as a theorem)</h4>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Under the McGucken Principle, the energy E of any isolated system is conserved:</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em><strong>dE/dt = 0.</strong></em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Proof.</strong> By Proposition IV.1, temporal uniformity of x₄&#8217;s advance implies time-translation invariance of the action. By Noether&#8217;s theorem (III.1) applied to time translation, the conserved charge is the Hamiltonian H = p_i q̇^i − L, which is the energy E. Therefore dE/dt = 0. ∎</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">IV.3 Momentum Conservation: The Standard Derivation</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The standard Noether derivation of momentum conservation takes spatial-translation invariance of the action as input. For a Lagrangian density <strong>L</strong>(φ, ∂φ) with no explicit dependence on position <strong>x</strong>, the infinitesimal transformation x^i → x^i + ε^i leaves <strong>L</strong> invariant, and the Noether current is the stress-energy tensor</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">T^{μν} = (∂<strong>L</strong>/∂(∂_μφ)) ∂^νφ − η^{μν} <strong>L</strong>,</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">whose spatial components yield the conserved three-momentum</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">P^i = ∫ T^{0i} d³x, dP^i/dt = 0. (IV.2)</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The derivation requires that the Lagrangian have no explicit <strong>x</strong>-dependence. In the standard presentation this is taken as empirical: space is homogeneous, no position is preferred, so the Lagrangian is translation invariant. The McGucken framework supplies the geometric origin.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">IV.4 The Geometric Antecedent: Spatial Homogeneity of x₄&#8217;s Expansion</h3>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading">Proposition IV.3 (Spatial homogeneity of x₄&#8217;s expansion as translation invariance)</h4>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Under the McGucken Principle, the following are equivalent:</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>(a) The action is invariant under the infinitesimal spatial translation x^i → x^i + ε^i for every ε^i ∈ ℝ;</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>(b) x₄ advances at rate ic from every spacetime event equally — no spatial point is a preferred source of x₄&#8217;s expansion.</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Proof.</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>(b) ⇒ (a).</em> By Postulate 1, dx₄/dt = ic at every event, with the advance proceeding spherically symmetrically about each event. This spherical symmetry around each point includes, as a subcase, the translational symmetry that x₄&#8217;s advance is the same process at every spatial location. Consider two events p₁ = (<strong>x</strong>₁, t) and p₂ = (<strong>x</strong>₂, t) at the same coordinate time but different spatial positions, with <strong>x</strong>₂ = <strong>x</strong>₁ + <strong>ε</strong>. By Postulate 1, x₄&#8217;s advance from p₁ is spherically symmetric about <strong>x</strong>₁, and x₄&#8217;s advance from p₂ is spherically symmetric about <strong>x</strong>₂. The two processes are related by the translation <strong>x</strong>₁ → <strong>x</strong>₁ + <strong>ε</strong>; the structure of x₄&#8217;s advance is identical at both points. A worldline γ and its translate γ&#8217; = γ + <strong>ε</strong> traverse the same integrated x₄-advance. Therefore S[γ&#8217;] = S[γ], which is the statement of translation invariance.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>(a) ⇒ (b).</em> If x₄&#8217;s advance differed in character at different spatial locations — for instance, if the rate were dx₄/dt = if(<strong>x</strong>)c with f(<strong>x</strong>) ≠ 1 — then the action would acquire explicit <strong>x</strong>-dependence through the measure, and δS under x^i → x^i + ε^i would not vanish. Invariance for arbitrary γ and arbitrary <strong>ε</strong> forces the advance to be spatially uniform: the process dx₄/dt = ic at every event, with no preferred spatial position. ∎</p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading">Proposition IV.4 (Momentum conservation as a theorem)</h4>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Under the McGucken Principle, the three-momentum P^i of any isolated system is conserved:</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em><strong>dP^i/dt = 0.</strong></em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Proof.</strong> By Proposition IV.3, spatial homogeneity of x₄&#8217;s expansion implies translation invariance of the action. By Noether&#8217;s theorem (III.1) applied to spatial translations, the conserved charge is the spatial momentum P^i of (IV.2). Therefore dP^i/dt = 0. ∎</p>
