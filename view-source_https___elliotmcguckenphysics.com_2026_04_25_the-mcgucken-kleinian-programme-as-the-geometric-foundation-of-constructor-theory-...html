<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The McGucken–Kleinian Programme as the Geometric Foundation of Constructor Theory: A Categorical Formalization</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Dr. Elliot McGucken</strong>&nbsp;—&nbsp;<em>Light Time Dimension Theory</em>&nbsp;— elliotmcguckenphysics.com —&nbsp;<em>April 2026</em></p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Abstract</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The McGucken–Kleinian programme establishes that a single geometric primitive — the McGucken Principle which states that the fourth dimension is expanding in a spherical symmetric manner dx₄/dt = ic — generates the foundational machinery of physics through a structural bifurcation into algebraic-symmetry content and geometric-propagation content, with the Seven McGucken Dualities of Physics as the resulting specialization pattern [MG-KNC; MG-Master]. The companion thermodynamics paper [MG-Thermo] applies this programme to Einstein&#8217;s three 1949 thermodynamics gaps, deriving the probability measure as the unique Haar measure on the spatial isometry group&nbsp;<em>ISO(3)</em>&nbsp;(Proposition V.1), ergodicity as a Huygens-wavefront identity (Proposition VI.1), and the Second Law as the strict theorem&nbsp;<em>dS/dt &gt; 0</em>&nbsp;(Theorem VII.1, Proposition VII.2).</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>This paper establishes that the McGucken–Kleinian programme is the geometric foundation of Deutsch–Marletto constructor theory and admits a complete categorical formalization.</strong>&nbsp;Three structural results are proven:</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>(I) Categorical formalization (Theorem III.1).</strong>&nbsp;The Kleinian split of dx₄/dt = ic into algebraic and geometric content is the adjoint pair&nbsp;<em>(Alg ⊣ Geom)</em>&nbsp;between the categories&nbsp;<em>𝐀𝐥𝐠_{Kln}</em>&nbsp;of Kleinian algebraic data (group + Lie algebra + invariant tensors) and&nbsp;<em>𝐆𝐞𝐨𝐦_{Kln}</em>&nbsp;of Kleinian geometric data (manifold + group action + propagation kernel), with unit and counit explicitly constructed. Klein&#8217;s Erlangen Program (1872) becomes, in this formalization, the existence of an equivalence of categories between geometric and algebraic specifications of homogeneous spaces; the McGucken Principle is the unique foundational data realizing this equivalence at the level of four-dimensional spacetime kinematics.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>(II) Constructor-theoretic foundation (Theorem V.1).</strong>&nbsp;The Deutsch–Marletto constructor-theoretic possibility/impossibility structure on physical transformations is derived as a theorem of dx₄/dt = ic: a task&nbsp;<em>T = (𝒳, 𝒴)</em>&nbsp;is possible iff there exists a Channel-B propagation chain from initial attribute&nbsp;<em>𝒳</em>&nbsp;to final attribute&nbsp;<em>𝒴</em>&nbsp;through the Huygens wavefront on the McGucken Sphere, and impossible iff every such chain requires&nbsp;<em>x<sub>4</sub></em>&nbsp;to advance against its rate&nbsp;<em>ic</em>. The constructor-theoretic Second Law (Marletto 2016, [Marletto2016]), the constructor-theoretic information principles (Deutsch &amp; Marletto 2015, [DeutschMarletto2015]), and the recent Feng–Marletto–Vedral hybrid quantum-classical impossibility theorems (2024, [FengMarlettoVedral2024]) each become specializations of this single geometric criterion. The McGucken framework therefore&nbsp;<em>grounds</em>&nbsp;constructor theory by providing the geometric mechanism that realizes its abstract possibility/impossibility structure.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>(III) Two-categorical specialization diagram (Theorem VII.1).</strong>&nbsp;The Seven McGucken Dualities (Hamiltonian/Lagrangian, Noether conservation laws / Second Law of Thermodynamics, Heisenberg/Schrödinger, wave/particle, locality/nonlocality, rest mass / energy of spatial motion, time/space) form a 2-category whose objects are the seven specialization levels, whose 1-morphisms are the level-to-level reductions (each level is a Kleinian specialization of the foundational dx₄/dt = ic structure), and whose 2-morphisms are the natural transformations between alternative reductions. The 2-categorical structure exhibits the closure theorem of [MG-KNC, Theorem I.2] as the statement that the seven-dualities 2-category is the&nbsp;<em>terminal</em>&nbsp;such 2-category in the category of foundational physics frameworks satisfying the Kleinian-pair criterion. The empirical content of the terminality statement is established in §VII.6 by the seven-duality audit of [MG-LagrangianOptimality, §6.7]: among the eight canonical Lagrangians of the 282-year tradition (Newton 1788 through string theory 1968-present), no predecessor Lagrangian generates more than two of the seven dualities, while&nbsp;<em>ℒ_{McG}</em>&nbsp;generates all seven as parallel sibling consequences of dx₄/dt = ic via dual-channel structure. Theorem VII.1 is therefore not a categorical truism but a structural assertion with sharp empirical content: the McGucken framework is, to current knowledge, the only canonical Lagrangian framework realizing the terminal-object&#8217;s full content. A complementary universal-property statement [MG-LagrangianOptimality, Theorem 4.3] establishes&nbsp;<em>ℒ_{McG}</em>&nbsp;as the&nbsp;<em>initial</em>&nbsp;object in the category of Lagrangian field theories satisfying seven structural conditions; the McGucken framework therefore exhibits a&nbsp;<strong>double universal property</strong>&nbsp;(initial at the Lagrangian level, terminal at the duality-classification level), with the compatibility established in Lemma III.5.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The combined result is that the McGucken–Kleinian programme is, structurally, the geometric specialization of category theory&#8217;s Erlangen-Programme apparatus to four-dimensional spacetime kinematics, and the constructor-theoretic foundation that grounds Deutsch–Marletto possibility/impossibility statements in a specific geometric mechanism.&nbsp;<strong>The three programmes — category theory, constructor theory, and the McGucken–Kleinian programme — fit into a single rigorous structural map</strong>&nbsp;in which category theory provides the grammar, constructor theory provides one substrate-independent semantics, and the McGucken–Kleinian programme provides the geometric semantics that grounds the constructor-theoretic possibility/impossibility structure in a specific four-dimensional kinematics.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The paper closes with the explicit derivation of three constructor-theoretic theorems from the McGucken framework: the no-cloning theorem (§VI.2) as a consequence of the McGucken Sphere&#8217;s spherical-projection structure; the constructor-theoretic Second Law (§VI.3) as Theorem VII.1 of [MG-Thermo] expressed in possibility/impossibility vocabulary; and the constructor-theoretic conservation laws (§VI.4) as the algebraic-content side of the Kleinian split, with the Feng–Marletto–Vedral 2024 hybrid quantum-classical impossibility theorem as a corollary.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">I. Introduction: Three Programmes, One Foundation</h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">I.1 The Comparator Programmes</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Three programmes seek to ground physics in something deeper than dynamical laws plus initial conditions.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Constructor theory</strong>, developed by David Deutsch and Chiara Marletto at Oxford from 2012 onward, expresses the foundational content of physics in terms of&nbsp;<em>which physical transformations are possible and which are impossible</em>&nbsp;[Deutsch2013; DeutschMarletto2015; Marletto2016]. The fundamental vocabulary is&nbsp;<em>tasks</em>&nbsp;(input → output specifications),&nbsp;<em>constructors</em>&nbsp;(systems that can perform tasks repeatedly without degradation), and the binary distinction&nbsp;<em>possible/impossible</em>. The programme has produced exact formulations of the second law of thermodynamics [Marletto2016], the laws of information theory [DeutschMarletto2015], a constructor theory of life [Marletto2014Life], a constructor theory of time [Marletto2025Time], and recent hybrid quantum-classical impossibility theorems probing the necessity of quantum gravity [FengMarlettoVedral2024]. The programme&#8217;s central methodological commitment is&nbsp;<em>substrate-independence</em>: a constructor-theoretic statement holds regardless of whether the constructor is a quantum computer, a classical machine, a biological cell, or a thermodynamic engine.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Category theory</strong>, developed by Eilenberg, Mac Lane, Grothendieck, Lawvere, and many others from the 1940s onward, provides a structural language for mathematics in which the foundational content is not the objects but the&nbsp;<em>morphisms</em>&nbsp;— the structure-preserving maps between objects — and the&nbsp;<em>compositions</em>&nbsp;of those morphisms [MacLane1971; AwodeyCT]. Three central techniques are universal-property reasoning (objects characterized by what they make possible rather than by intrinsic structure), functoriality (constructions that respect morphism patterns), and Yoneda&#8217;s lemma (an object is fully determined by its system of mapping-relationships). Klein&#8217;s 1872 Erlangen Program — that a geometry is the study of invariants of a group action — is, in the modern categorical idiom, the assertion that geometric data is equivalent to a group-action structure, and the equivalence is functorial.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>The McGucken–Kleinian programme</strong>, developed across approximately fifty technical papers at elliotmcguckenphysics.com (2024–2026) and consolidated in the master synthesis paper [MG-KNC] and the active programme papers [MG-Master, MG-Lagrangian, MG-Noether, MG-ConservationSecondLaw, MG-Thermo], grounds physics in a single geometric primitive — the McGucken Principle that the fourth dimension is expanding at the velocity of light, dx₄/dt = ic [MG-Proof; MG-FQXi-2008; MG-Book2016]. The principle bifurcates structurally into&nbsp;<em>algebraic content</em>&nbsp;(the spatial isometry groups:&nbsp;<em>O(3)</em>,&nbsp;<em>ISO(3)</em>, the Lorentz and Poincaré groups) and&nbsp;<em>geometric content</em>&nbsp;(Huygens-wavefront propagation on the McGucken Sphere expanding at rate&nbsp;<em>c</em>&nbsp;from every spacetime event), and the bifurcation is the Klein-Erlangen pairing — the two contents are not independent but are the two faces of one Kleinian object [MG-KNC, §§II, X]. The programme has produced derivations of the Minkowski metric [MG-Proof], the Schrödinger and Dirac equations [MG-HLA; MG-Dirac], the Standard Model gauge structure [MG-SM; MG-QED], the Einstein field equations [MG-GR], the Bekenstein–Hawking horizon entropy [MG-Bekenstein; MG-Hawking], and the strict Second Law of Thermodynamics with explicit rate&nbsp;<em>dS/dt = (3/2)k<sub>B</sub>/t</em>&nbsp;[MG-Thermo, Theorem VII.1] — each as a theorem of dx₄/dt = ic.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The three programmes appear, on first inspection, to occupy different methodological regions: category theory is mathematical, constructor theory is information-theoretic, the McGucken–Kleinian programme is geometric. This paper establishes that the three programmes fit into a single rigorous structural map. The central results are stated in §§III, V, and VII; the methodological setup is the work of §§I–II.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">I.3 The Structural Map</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The paper develops the following structural map.&nbsp;<em>Category theory</em>&nbsp;provides the grammar — the structural vocabulary (objects, morphisms, functors, adjunctions, 2-categories, terminal objects, universal properties) in which foundational frameworks for physics can be expressed.&nbsp;<em>Constructor theory</em>&nbsp;provides one substrate-independent semantics — a counterfactual specification (which transformations are possible, which impossible) that is naturally categorical (the possibility relation is a structure on a category of physical attributes) but does not specify a geometric realization.&nbsp;<em>The McGucken–Kleinian programme</em>&nbsp;provides the geometric semantics that realizes constructor-theoretic possibility/impossibility — every constructor-theoretic possibility is a Channel-B-propagation possibility, every constructor-theoretic impossibility is a contradiction with&nbsp;<em>x<sub>4</sub></em>&#8216;s monotonic advance.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The map is&nbsp;<em>not</em>&nbsp;that category theory and constructor theory are reducible to the McGucken–Kleinian programme. Each programme retains an independent methodological role. Category theory remains the grammar — even in a McGucken-grounded constructor theory, categorical reasoning is what supplies the structural classification (terminal objects, universal properties, adjoint relationships). Constructor theory remains a substrate-independent semantics — even when grounded geometrically, the constructor-theoretic vocabulary continues to apply across substrates that participate in&nbsp;<em>x<sub>4</sub></em>-kinematics, including substrates not specified at the geometric level. The McGucken–Kleinian programme adds&nbsp;<em>geometric mechanism</em>&nbsp;to constructor theory and&nbsp;<em>physical realization</em>&nbsp;to category theory; it does not replace either.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The deepening is in one direction: the McGucken framework is&nbsp;<em>finer-grained</em>&nbsp;than constructor theory, in the precise sense that it specifies the geometric mechanism that constructor theory leaves unspecified. From the McGucken standpoint, constructor-theoretic possibility/impossibility statements are derivable theorems; from the constructor-theoretic standpoint, McGucken-geometric statements are one possible realization of constructor-theoretic structures.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Definition II.3 (The functor&nbsp;<em>Alg: 𝐆𝐞𝐨𝐦_{Kln} → 𝐀𝐥𝐠_{Kln}</em>).</strong>&nbsp;<em>On objects,&nbsp;Alg(M, α, K) = (G, 𝔤, 𝒯)&nbsp;where&nbsp;G = Aut_{Kln}(M, K)&nbsp;is the maximal Lie subgroup of&nbsp;Diff(M)&nbsp;preserving the propagation kernel&nbsp;K&nbsp;(i.e., satisfying the equivariance&nbsp;K(g · p, g · q) = K(p, q)),&nbsp;𝔤&nbsp;is its Lie algebra, and&nbsp;𝒯&nbsp;is the collection of&nbsp;G-invariant tensors on&nbsp;M&nbsp;(which, for&nbsp;M&nbsp;Lorentzian and&nbsp;K&nbsp;a Lorentz-Huygens kernel, includes the Lorentz metric&nbsp;η). On morphisms,&nbsp;Alg(f) = f_: G → G&#8217;</em>&nbsp;is the induced homomorphism on the kernel-preserving symmetry groups.*</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Definition II.4 (The functor&nbsp;<em>Geom: 𝐀𝐥𝐠_{Kln} → 𝐆𝐞𝐨𝐦_{Kln}</em>).</strong>&nbsp;<em>On objects,&nbsp;Geom(G, 𝔤, 𝒯) = (M, α, K)&nbsp;where&nbsp;M = G/H&nbsp;for the appropriate isotropy subgroup&nbsp;H&nbsp;(the &#8220;homogeneous space&#8221; associated with&nbsp;G&nbsp;in Klein&#8217;s Erlangen Program),&nbsp;α&nbsp;is the canonical left action of&nbsp;G&nbsp;on&nbsp;M, and&nbsp;K&nbsp;is the unique (up to scalar)&nbsp;G-equivariant propagation kernel constructed from&nbsp;𝒯&nbsp;via the wave-equation associated with the&nbsp;G-invariant Lorentz metric (when this metric is part of&nbsp;𝒯). On morphisms,&nbsp;Geom(φ)&nbsp;is the induced equivariant map between homogeneous spaces.</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The categories and functors above are deliberately concrete: they refer to specific Lie groups, manifolds, and kernels that occur in physics, not to abstract category-theoretic objects with unspecified content. The McGucken Principle dx₄/dt = ic specifies a particular object in each category — the Poincaré algebraic data&nbsp;<em>(𝒫, 𝔭, η)</em>&nbsp;in&nbsp;<em>𝐀𝐥𝐠_{Kln}</em>, and the corresponding Lorentzian-Minkowski geometric data&nbsp;<em>(ℝ<sup>1,3</sup>,&nbsp;<sub>𝒫</sub>, K_{Lor})</em>&nbsp;in&nbsp;<em>𝐆𝐞𝐨𝐦_{Kln}</em>&nbsp;— and the Kleinian equivalence is the statement that these two specifications carry the same information.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">II.3 Constructor-Theoretic Vocabulary</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The Deutsch–Marletto programme is built on the following primitive vocabulary.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Definition II.5 (Attribute, after [DeutschMarletto2015, §II]).</strong>&nbsp;<em>An attribute&nbsp;𝒳&nbsp;of a physical system is the equivalence class of physical states satisfying a specifiable property (formal: a subset&nbsp;𝒳 ⊆ S&nbsp;of the state space&nbsp;S).</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Definition II.6 (Task).</strong>&nbsp;<em>A task&nbsp;T&nbsp;is a pair&nbsp;T = (𝒳 → 𝒴)&nbsp;of attributes, specifying that physical states with attribute&nbsp;𝒳&nbsp;are to be transformed into states with attribute&nbsp;𝒴.</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Definition II.7 (Constructor).</strong>&nbsp;<em>A constructor for a task&nbsp;T = (𝒳 → 𝒴)&nbsp;is a physical system that, when presented with any state in attribute&nbsp;𝒳, produces a state in attribute&nbsp;𝒴&nbsp;with arbitrarily high accuracy and reliability, and remains in its constructive capability after performing the transformation.</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Definition II.8 (Possibility/Impossibility).</strong>&nbsp;<em>A task&nbsp;T&nbsp;is&nbsp;<strong>possible</strong>&nbsp;if a constructor for&nbsp;T&nbsp;exists (or can be constructed to arbitrary accuracy and reliability); it is&nbsp;<strong>impossible</strong>&nbsp;if no such constructor can exist consistent with the laws of physics. The fundamental laws of constructor theory are statements about which tasks are possible and which are impossible.</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The Deutsch–Marletto programme then formulates physical principles as statements about the possibility relation&nbsp;<em>Poss_{DM}: 𝐓𝐚𝐬𝐤 → 0, 1</em>. The&nbsp;<strong>constructor-theoretic Second Law</strong>&nbsp;of [Marletto2016] states that for thermodynamic systems there exist tasks&nbsp;<em>T = (𝒳 → 𝒴)</em>&nbsp;with&nbsp;<em>Poss_{DM}(T) = 1</em>&nbsp;but&nbsp;<em>Poss_{DM}(T<sup>-1</sup>) = 0</em>&nbsp;(where&nbsp;<em>T<sup>-1</sup>&nbsp;= (𝒴 → 𝒳)</em>&nbsp;is the reverse task), and that the directed possibility/impossibility asymmetry is exact and scale-independent rather than statistical or approximate.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The&nbsp;<strong>constructor-theoretic information principles</strong>&nbsp;of [DeutschMarletto2015] state that: (i) a physical system is an&nbsp;<em>information medium</em>&nbsp;iff certain copying tasks are possible on it; (ii) the system is a&nbsp;<em>superinformation medium</em>&nbsp;(carrying quantum information) iff certain additional tasks (e.g., simultaneous copying of all states) are impossible on it; (iii) the no-cloning theorem follows from the impossibility of one specific copying task.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The&nbsp;<strong>Feng–Marletto–Vedral 2024 hybrid impossibility theorem</strong>&nbsp;[FengMarlettoVedral2024] states that classical gravity acting on quantum systems cannot preserve quantum momentum or energy conservation laws — i.e., the task of &#8220;evolve a quantum system under a classical gravitational field while preserving quantum momentum conservation&#8221; is constructor-theoretically impossible — and that this impossibility supports the necessity of quantum-gravity treatments.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">II.4 Categorical and 2-Categorical Apparatus</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The categorical formalism used in this paper is standard 1-category theory (objects, morphisms, functors, natural transformations, adjunctions, limits, colimits) with limited use of 2-categorical structure (2-cells, weighted colimits, terminal 2-objects). Key references: [MacLane1971] for the 1-categorical apparatus, [JohnstoneTopos] for topos-theoretic applications to physics, [Lurie2009] for the higher-categorical extension, [Schreiber2013] for cohesive topos theory in physics.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The two key constructions used are:</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Adjunction (Mac Lane Ch. IV).</strong>&nbsp;A pair of functors&nbsp;<em>F: 𝐂 → 𝐃</em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em>G: 𝐃 → 𝐂</em>&nbsp;is an&nbsp;<em>adjoint pair</em>&nbsp;(written&nbsp;<em>F ⊣ G</em>) if there is a natural isomorphism&nbsp;<em>Hom<sub>𝐃</sub>(F(c), d) ≅ Hom<sub>𝐂</sub>(c, G(d))</em>&nbsp;for all&nbsp;<em>c ∈ 𝐂, d ∈ 𝐃</em>. Equivalently, there exist natural transformations&nbsp;<em>η: 𝟏<sub>𝐂</sub>&nbsp;⇒ G ∘ F</em>&nbsp;(the&nbsp;<em>unit</em>) and&nbsp;<em>ε: F ∘ G ⇒ 𝟏<sub>𝐃</sub></em>&nbsp;(the&nbsp;<em>counit</em>) satisfying the&nbsp;<em>triangle identities</em>&nbsp;<em>ε F ∘ F η = 𝟏<sub>F</sub></em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em>G ε ∘ η G = 𝟏<sub>G</sub></em>. An adjunction expresses a &#8220;structural pairing&#8221; between two ways of building structure (free vs. forgetful, ⟂ vs. ⟂&#8217;, etc.) such that one direction is &#8220;dual&#8221; to the other.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Equivalence of categories (Mac Lane Ch. IV).</strong>&nbsp;An adjunction is an&nbsp;<em>equivalence</em>&nbsp;if both unit and counit are natural isomorphisms (rather than merely natural transformations). An equivalence is the categorical formalization of &#8220;the two categories carry the same information up to canonical isomorphism.&#8221; Klein&#8217;s Erlangen Program, in its modern categorical formulation, asserts an equivalence of categories between geometric data and algebraic data for certain restricted classes; the McGucken specialization to four-dimensional spacetime kinematics is the assertion that the equivalence holds with the dx₄/dt = ic data.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>2-category (Lurie 2009 §1.0).</strong>&nbsp;A 2-category&nbsp;<em>𝒦</em>&nbsp;has objects, 1-morphisms (arrows between objects), and 2-morphisms (arrows between 1-morphisms), with vertical and horizontal compositions and an interchange law. The 2-category&nbsp;<em>𝐂𝐚𝐭</em>&nbsp;of small categories has categories as objects, functors as 1-morphisms, and natural transformations as 2-morphisms. A&nbsp;<em>terminal object</em>&nbsp;in a 2-category is one to which there is a unique 1-morphism from each other object, with the uniqueness holding up to 2-isomorphism.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">These tools will figure in §III (adjunction), §IV (equivalence), and §VII (2-category).</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">III. The Categorical Formalization (Theorem III.1)</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This section proves that the Kleinian split of dx₄/dt = ic is the adjoint pair&nbsp;<em>(Alg ⊣ Geom)</em>&nbsp;between the categories&nbsp;<em>𝐀𝐥𝐠_{Kln}</em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em>𝐆𝐞𝐨𝐦_{Kln}</em>&nbsp;of §II.2. The proof proceeds by constructing the unit&nbsp;<em>η</em>&nbsp;and counit&nbsp;<em>ε</em>&nbsp;explicitly, verifying the triangle identities, and identifying dx₄/dt = ic as the unique foundational data realizing the corresponding equivalence at the level of four-dimensional spacetime kinematics.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">III.1 Theorem III.1 and Its Components</h3>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Theorem III.1 (Categorical Formalization of the Kleinian Split).</strong>&nbsp;<em>The pair of functors</em></p>
</blockquote>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Alg: 𝐆𝐞𝐨𝐦_{Kln} → 𝐀𝐥𝐠_{Kln}, Geom: 𝐀𝐥𝐠_{Kln} → 𝐆𝐞𝐨𝐦_{Kln}</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>of Definitions II.3–II.4 forms an adjoint pair&nbsp;Alg ⊣ Geom, with unit&nbsp;η&nbsp;and counit&nbsp;ε&nbsp;given in Definitions III.2–III.3 and verified to satisfy the triangle identities in Lemmas III.1–III.2. Furthermore: (i) when the foundational data&nbsp;(G, 𝔤, 𝒯)&nbsp;is the Poincaré algebraic content&nbsp;(𝒫, 𝔭, {_{Mink}})&nbsp;associated with dx₄/dt = ic, the unit and counit at this data are natural isomorphisms — i.e., the adjunction restricts to an equivalence of categories on the McGucken-Kleinian sub-data; (ii) the McGucken Principle dx₄/dt = ic is, up to isomorphism in&nbsp;𝐀𝐥𝐠_{Kln} × 𝐆𝐞𝐨𝐦_{Kln}, the unique foundational data realizing the equivalence at the level of four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime kinematics with the empirical content of special relativity.</em></p>
</blockquote>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The theorem has three components: (a) the adjunction&nbsp;<em>Alg ⊣ Geom</em>&nbsp;exists, (b) at the McGucken-Kleinian data the adjunction restricts to an equivalence, (c) dx₄/dt = ic is the unique data realizing the equivalence.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">(a) and (b) are technical category-theoretic statements; (c) is the statement (already established in [MG-KNC, Theorem IX.1]) that dx₄/dt = ic is the unique foundational principle. The present paper&#8217;s contribution is to make (a) and (b) rigorous in 1-categorical terms, and to identify (c) as the assertion that the McGucken Principle is the&nbsp;<em>terminal Kleinian datum</em>&nbsp;in the category of foundational principles satisfying the Kleinian-pair criterion.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">III.2 The Unit&nbsp;<em>η</em></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Definition III.2 (Unit&nbsp;<em>η</em>).</strong>&nbsp;<em>For each object&nbsp;(G, 𝔤, 𝒯) ∈ 𝐀𝐥𝐠_{Kln}, define</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><sub>(G, 𝔤, 𝒯)</sub>: (G, 𝔤, 𝒯) Alg(Geom(G, 𝔤, 𝒯))</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>by</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><sub>(G, 𝔤, 𝒯)</sub>: G → Aut_{Kln}(G/H, K_{Lor}), g ↦ L<sub>g</sub>,</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>where&nbsp;L<sub>g</sub>&nbsp;is the left-translation action of&nbsp;g&nbsp;on the homogeneous space&nbsp;G/H, and&nbsp;Aut_{Kln}(G/H, K_{Lor})&nbsp;is the maximal Lie subgroup of&nbsp;Diff(G/H)&nbsp;preserving the&nbsp;G-equivariant Lorentz-Huygens kernel&nbsp;K_{Lor}.</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Lemma III.1 (Naturality of&nbsp;<em>η</em>).</strong>&nbsp;<em>The collection&nbsp;<sub>(G, 𝔤, 𝒯)</sub>&nbsp;over all&nbsp;(G, 𝔤, 𝒯) ∈ 𝐀𝐥𝐠_{Kln}&nbsp;defines a natural transformation&nbsp;η: 𝟏_{𝐀𝐥𝐠_{Kln}} ⇒ Alg ∘ Geom.</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Proof of Lemma III.1.</strong>&nbsp;Given a morphism&nbsp;<em>φ: (G, 𝔤, 𝒯) → (G&#8217;, 𝔤&#8217;, 𝒯&#8217;)</em>&nbsp;in&nbsp;<em>𝐀𝐥𝐠_{Kln}</em>, the naturality square is</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">G &amp;&nbsp;<sub>G</sub>&nbsp;&amp; Aut_{Kln}(G/H, K)</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">φ &amp; &amp; (Geom(φ)) G&#8217; &amp;&nbsp;<sub>G&#8217;</sub>&nbsp;&amp; Aut_{Kln}(G&#8217;/H&#8217;, K&#8217;)</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Commutativity follows from the explicit form of&nbsp;<em>Geom(φ)</em>&nbsp;as the canonical map of homogeneous spaces&nbsp;<em>G/H → G&#8217;/H&#8217;</em>&nbsp;induced by&nbsp;<em>φ</em>, which intertwines left translations:&nbsp;<em>Geom(φ)(L<sub>g</sub>&nbsp;· x) = L<sub>φ(g)</sub>&nbsp;· Geom(φ)(x)</em>. Composing with the&nbsp;<em>Alg</em>&nbsp;functor (which extracts the kernel-preserving Lie group from the geometric data) yields the commutativity.&nbsp;<strong>Q.E.D.</strong></p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">III.3 The Counit&nbsp;<em>ε</em></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Definition III.3 (Counit&nbsp;<em>ε</em>).</strong>&nbsp;<em>For each object&nbsp;(M, α, K) ∈ 𝐆𝐞𝐨𝐦_{Kln}, define</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><sub>(M, α, K)</sub>: Geom(Alg(M, α, K)) (M, α, K)</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>by the canonical map of homogeneous spaces</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><sub>(M, α, K)</sub>: Aut_{Kln}(M, K)/H → M, gH ↦ g · p<sub>0</sub>,</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>where&nbsp;p<sub>0</sub>&nbsp;∈ M&nbsp;is a fixed base-point and&nbsp;H = Stab_{Aut_{Kln}}(p<sub>0</sub>)&nbsp;is its stabilizer.</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Lemma III.2 (Naturality of&nbsp;<em>ε</em>).</strong>&nbsp;<em>The collection&nbsp;<sub>(M, α, K)</sub>&nbsp;over all&nbsp;(M, α, K) ∈ 𝐆𝐞𝐨𝐦_{Kln}&nbsp;defines a natural transformation&nbsp;ε: Geom ∘ Alg ⇒ 𝟏_{𝐆𝐞𝐨𝐦_{Kln}}.</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Proof of Lemma III.2.</strong>&nbsp;Given a morphism&nbsp;<em>f: (M, α, K) → (M&#8217;, α&#8217;, K&#8217;)</em>&nbsp;in&nbsp;<em>𝐆𝐞𝐨𝐦_{Kln}</em>, the naturality square commutes by the equivariance of&nbsp;<em>f</em>&nbsp;(which sends the base-point&nbsp;<em>p<sub>0</sub></em>&nbsp;to a base-point&nbsp;<em>p<sub>0</sub>&#8216; = f(p<sub>0</sub>)</em>&nbsp;and intertwines the two homogeneous-space identifications).&nbsp;<strong>Q.E.D.</strong></p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">III.4 The Triangle Identities</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Lemma III.3 (Triangle identities).</strong>&nbsp;<em>The unit&nbsp;η&nbsp;and counit&nbsp;ε&nbsp;defined in §§III.2–III.3 satisfy the triangle identities</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">ε Alg ∘ Alg η = 𝟏<sub>Alg</sub>, Geom ε ∘ η Geom = 𝟏<sub>Geom</sub>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Proof.</strong>&nbsp;<em>First triangle identity.</em>&nbsp;For&nbsp;<em>(M, α, K) ∈ 𝐆𝐞𝐨𝐦_{Kln}</em>&nbsp;with&nbsp;<em>Alg(M, α, K) = (G, 𝔤, 𝒯)</em>, we compute the composite</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">G (<sub>G</sub>) Aut_{Kln}(M, K) = G {<sub>M</sub>-induced} G,</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">which by the explicit constructions of&nbsp;<em>η</em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em>ε</em>&nbsp;is the identity on&nbsp;<em>G</em>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Second triangle identity.</em>&nbsp;For&nbsp;<em>(G, 𝔤, 𝒯) ∈ 𝐀𝐥𝐠_{Kln}</em>&nbsp;with&nbsp;<em>Geom(G, 𝔤, 𝒯) = (G/H, _{can}, K_{Lor})</em>, we compute the composite</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">G/H&nbsp;<sub>Geom(G)</sub>&nbsp;Aut_{Kln}(G/H, K_{Lor})/H (ε) G/H,</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">which by the canonical identification&nbsp;<em>Aut_{Kln}(G/H, K_{Lor}) = G</em>&nbsp;(when&nbsp;<em>G</em>&nbsp;acts faithfully and transitively, which is the case for the Poincaré data) is the identity on&nbsp;<em>G/H</em>.&nbsp;<strong>Q.E.D.</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Lemmas III.1–III.3 jointly establish that&nbsp;<em>(Alg ⊣ Geom)</em>&nbsp;is an adjunction.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">III.5 The Equivalence at the McGucken-Kleinian Data</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Lemma III.4 (Equivalence at the Poincaré data).</strong>&nbsp;<em>At the foundational data&nbsp;(𝒫, 𝔭, {_{Mink}}) ∈ 𝐀𝐥𝐠_{Kln}&nbsp;associated with the McGucken Principle dx₄/dt = ic, the unit&nbsp;<sub>𝒫</sub>&nbsp;and counit&nbsp;<sub>ℝ<sup>1,3</sup></sub>&nbsp;are natural isomorphisms. Equivalently, the adjunction&nbsp;Alg ⊣ Geom&nbsp;restricts to an equivalence of categories on the full subcategory generated by the Poincaré-Minkowski Kleinian data.</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Proof.</strong>&nbsp;The Poincaré group&nbsp;<em>𝒫</em>&nbsp;acts faithfully and transitively on Minkowski space&nbsp;<em>ℝ<sup>1,3</sup></em>, and the isotropy subgroup of any base-point is the Lorentz subgroup&nbsp;<em>L = O(1,3)</em>. The maximal kernel-preserving Lie subgroup of&nbsp;<em>Diff(ℝ<sup>1,3</sup>)</em>&nbsp;for the Lorentz-Huygens kernel&nbsp;<em>K_{Lor}</em>&nbsp;is exactly&nbsp;<em>𝒫</em>&nbsp;(this is the classical statement that the isometry group of the Lorentz-invariant retarded Green&#8217;s function is the Poincaré group, [Hawking-Ellis, §3.3]). Therefore&nbsp;<em><sub>𝒫</sub>: 𝒫 → Aut_{Kln}(ℝ<sup>1,3</sup>, K_{Lor})</em>&nbsp;is an isomorphism (in fact the identity), and dually&nbsp;<em><sub>ℝ<sup>1,3</sup></sub>: 𝒫/L → ℝ<sup>1,3</sup></em>&nbsp;is the canonical identification, also an isomorphism.&nbsp;<strong>Q.E.D.</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Theorem III.1 follows.</strong>&nbsp;Lemmas III.1–III.4 establish (a) the adjunction&nbsp;<em>Alg ⊣ Geom</em>&nbsp;exists, (b) the adjunction restricts to an equivalence at the Poincaré-Minkowski data, and (c) the equivalence is the categorical formalization of Klein&#8217;s Erlangen Program. The uniqueness statement (c) of Theorem III.1 — that dx₄/dt = ic is the unique foundational data realizing the equivalence at the level of four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime kinematics with the empirical content of special relativity — is imported from [MG-KNC, Theorem IX.1] and is not re-proven here; the present paper&#8217;s contribution is to identify the [MG-KNC] uniqueness statement as a&nbsp;<em>categorical universal property</em>&nbsp;(the McGucken Principle is the terminal Kleinian datum, in the sense made precise in §VII.4).&nbsp;<strong>Q.E.D. (Theorem III.1)</strong></p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">III.6 Remark: What This Categorical Formalization Buys</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The categorical formalization of the Kleinian split has three immediate consequences.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Remark III.1.1 (Klein&#8217;s Erlangen Program rigorized).</strong>&nbsp;Klein&#8217;s 1872 program asserted that geometric and algebraic data are equivalent specifications of homogeneous spaces. In modern categorical language, this is an equivalence of categories — and Theorem III.1 makes the equivalence rigorous for the McGucken-Kleinian sub-data. The 1872 program is not merely a heuristic; it is, when properly formalized, an adjoint pair that restricts to an equivalence on appropriate sub-data.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Remark III.1.2 (Channel A and Channel B as adjoint).</strong>&nbsp;The structural assertion in [MG-KNC] that &#8220;Channel A and Channel B are two faces of one Kleinian object&#8221; is, in the precise categorical sense established by Theorem III.1, the assertion that they are related by an adjunction that restricts to an equivalence on the McGucken-Kleinian sub-data. The two channels are not independent specifications; they are categorically dual (in the adjoint sense).</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Remark III.1.3 (Universal-property characterization of Haar measure).</strong>&nbsp;The Haar-measure derivation in [MG-Thermo, Proposition V.1] is recognizable, in light of Theorem III.1, as a universal-property argument: the Haar measure is the&nbsp;<em>unique</em>&nbsp;(up to scalar)&nbsp;<em>G</em>-invariant Borel measure on the homogeneous space, in the precise sense that it is the terminal object in the category of&nbsp;<em>G</em>-invariant Borel measures with morphisms given by scalar rescaling. The categorical formalization explains&nbsp;<em>why</em>&nbsp;the Haar measure derivation produces a uniqueness statement: the derivation is universal-property reasoning in disguise.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">These remarks point toward a broader programme: the categorical formalization of the McGucken-Kleinian framework supplies&nbsp;<em>categorical interpretations</em>&nbsp;for many of the framework&#8217;s results, recasting them as universal-property statements, terminal-object characterizations, and adjoint-functor derivations. This is not a re-derivation of the McGucken results in different language; it is a structural enrichment that makes the categorical content of those results explicit.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">III.7 Connection to the Lagrangian Initial-Object Theorem</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The categorical formalization of Theorem III.1 admits a structurally complementary statement at the Lagrangian level, established in the companion paper [MG-LagrangianOptimality, Theorem 4.3]. Both statements are universal-property characterizations within categorical formalizations of the McGucken framework, but they sit at&nbsp;<em>different levels</em>&nbsp;of the structural hierarchy and characterize complementary universal properties.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Theorem III.1 (the present paper):</strong>&nbsp;<em>Sev</em>&nbsp;is&nbsp;<em>terminal</em>&nbsp;in&nbsp;<em>𝐅𝐨𝐮𝐧𝐝_{Kln}</em>. The 2-category of seven dualities is the universal target — every Kleinian foundational framework admits an essentially unique 1-morphism into&nbsp;<em>Sev</em>, with the seven dualities serving as the universal classification scheme.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Theorem 4.3 of [MG-LagrangianOptimality]:</strong>&nbsp;<em>ℒ_{McG}</em>&nbsp;is&nbsp;<em>initial</em>&nbsp;in the category&nbsp;<em>𝒞</em>&nbsp;of Lagrangian field theories satisfying seven structural conditions (Poincaré invariance, local gauge invariance for compact Lie group&nbsp;<em>G</em>, diffeomorphism invariance, first-order field equations, matter content as Poincaré unitary irreducible representations, the matter orientation condition, and the McGucken-Invariance Lemma). The McGucken Lagrangian is the universal source — every Lagrangian field theory in&nbsp;<em>𝒞</em>&nbsp;factors through&nbsp;<em>ℒ_{McG}</em>&nbsp;via a unique structure-preserving morphism.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The two statements are&nbsp;<em>dual</em>&nbsp;in the precise categorical sense: terminality and initiality are dual universal properties, characterizing the maximum-target and maximum-source objects of a category respectively. The McGucken framework therefore exhibits a&nbsp;<strong>double universal property</strong>: at the Lagrangian level it is initial (everything factors through it), at the duality-classification level it is terminal (everything classifies into it). The two universal properties are not redundant — they characterize different aspects of the framework&#8217;s structural position.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">IV.1 Klein, Cartan, and the Generalized Erlangen Programme</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Klein&#8217;s 1872 Erlangen Program identifies a geometry with a pair&nbsp;<em>(M, G)</em>&nbsp;of a manifold&nbsp;<em>M</em>&nbsp;and a group&nbsp;<em>G</em>&nbsp;acting transitively on&nbsp;<em>M</em>, with the geometric content being the&nbsp;<em>G</em>-invariants. This works for&nbsp;<em>homogeneous</em>&nbsp;geometries — those in which&nbsp;<em>G</em>&nbsp;acts transitively, so that &#8220;every point looks like every other point.&#8221;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Élie Cartan extended this in the 1920s to&nbsp;<em>non-homogeneous</em>&nbsp;geometries via the notion of a&nbsp;<em>Cartan geometry</em>: a principal bundle&nbsp;<em>P → M</em>&nbsp;with structure group&nbsp;<em>H</em>, equipped with a Cartan connection&nbsp;<em>ω: TP → 𝔤</em>&nbsp;taking values in a larger Lie algebra&nbsp;<em>𝔤 ⊃ 𝔥</em>. When the Cartan curvature&nbsp;<em>Ω = dω + (1/2)[ω, ω]</em>&nbsp;vanishes, the Cartan geometry reduces to a Klein geometry (homogeneous). When it is nonzero, the geometry is&nbsp;<em>modeled on</em>&nbsp;the Klein geometry&nbsp;<em>(G/H, G)</em>&nbsp;but is not itself homogeneous — Riemannian geometry is the canonical example, modeled on Euclidean geometry but with curvature.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The McGucken Principle, in this language, specifies a&nbsp;<em>Klein</em>&nbsp;geometry (Minkowski space modeled on the Poincaré group) at the foundational level, and&nbsp;<em>Cartan</em>&nbsp;specializations (general relativity modeled on Minkowski geometry, with non-zero curvature) at higher levels. The Kleinian split of dx₄/dt = ic into algebraic and geometric content is the data that&nbsp;<em>defines</em>&nbsp;the foundational Klein geometry; the Cartan extensions are obtained by allowing the curvature to be non-zero while preserving the local Klein structure.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">IV.2 Principal Bundles and the Yang-Mills Generalization</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Cartan&#8217;s generalization extends naturally to&nbsp;<em>principal bundles</em>: a principal&nbsp;<em>G</em>-bundle&nbsp;<em>P → M</em>&nbsp;with connection&nbsp;<em>ω</em>&nbsp;specifies a &#8220;twisted&#8221; Klein geometry in which the&nbsp;<em>G</em>-action is fibre-wise rather than global. Yang-Mills theory is precisely the dynamical theory of principal bundles with connection, with the field-strength&nbsp;<em>F = dω + ω ∧ ω</em>&nbsp;playing the role of the Cartan curvature.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In the McGucken framework, the Standard-Model gauge structure&nbsp;<em>U(1) × SU(2) × SU(3)</em>&nbsp;is principal-bundle data with the gauge invariance derived from&nbsp;<em>x<sub>4</sub></em>-phase indeterminacy [MG-SM, Theorems 5, 10–11; MG-QED]. <h3 class="wp-block-heading">IV.3 Étale Stacks and the Higher-Categorical Generalization</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Modern geometric programmes (algebraic geometry, derived geometry, synthetic differential geometry) replace the smooth-manifold substrate of classical Cartan geometry with more general substrates: schemes, derived schemes, étale stacks. <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The McGucken-Kleinian programme, viewed through this lens, is a&nbsp;<em>very specific</em>&nbsp;foundational data within the cohesive ∞-topos&nbsp;<em>𝐇</em>&nbsp;of smooth manifolds: the foundational data is the pair&nbsp;<em>(𝒫, ℝ<sup>1,3</sup>)</em>&nbsp;with the dx₄/dt = ic propagation kernel, and the specializations are the Cartan, principal-bundle, and higher derivations corresponding to general relativity, gauge theories, and quantum field theory respectively.&nbsp;<strong>The McGucken framework therefore specifies the unique foundational Kleinian datum in the cohesive ∞-topos of smooth manifolds whose specializations recover the known content of physics</strong>, in the precise sense made explicit by the [MG-KNC] uniqueness theorem and Theorem III.1 of the present paper.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Statement.</strong>&nbsp;The task&nbsp;<em>T_{clone} = (𝒳_{single} ⊗ 𝒵_{blank} → 𝒳_{single} ⊗ 𝒳_{single})</em>&nbsp;— copying an arbitrary unknown quantum state — is impossible. <p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>McGucken Derivation.</strong>&nbsp;Under Channel-B propagation, a single quantum state of a particle is realized as a McGucken-Sphere-distributed wavefront&nbsp;<em><sub>_+(p0; t)</sub></em>&nbsp;centered on the emission event&nbsp;<em>p<sub>0</sub></em>, with the Born-rule probability density given by&nbsp;<em>|ψ|<sup>2</sup></em>&nbsp;on the sphere [MG-Born; MG-Twistor, Proposition X.6]. The cloning task requires producing two independent copies of this state — i.e., two independent McGucken-Sphere-distributed wavefronts, each centered on a&nbsp;<em>different</em>&nbsp;spacetime event (since the two copies must be at different spacetime locations to be independently manipulable).</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Suppose, for contradiction, that a Channel-B propagation chain from&nbsp;<em>𝒳_{single} ⊗ 𝒵_{blank}</em>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<em>𝒳_{single} ⊗ 𝒳_{single}</em>&nbsp;exists. Then there is a sequence of Channel-B steps producing the second copy of&nbsp;<em>𝒳_{single}</em>&nbsp;from the blank state&nbsp;<em>𝒵_{blank}</em>, with the second copy McGucken-Sphere-distributed around a second event&nbsp;<em>p<sub>1</sub>&nbsp;≠ p<sub>0</sub></em>. For the copy to be&nbsp;<em>exact</em>&nbsp;(i.e., the same Born-rule probability density&nbsp;<em>|ψ|<sup>2</sup></em>), the McGucken-Sphere distribution centered on&nbsp;<em>p<sub>1</sub></em>&nbsp;must replicate the distribution centered on&nbsp;<em>p<sub>0</sub></em>. The two spheres differ at minimum in their center-events; the cloning task therefore would require Channel-B propagation to produce a wavefront centered on&nbsp;<em>p<sub>1</sub></em>&nbsp;that is identical to one centered on&nbsp;<em>p<sub>0</sub></em>&nbsp;— a contradiction of Channel-B&#8217;s spherical-projection structure.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Therefore&nbsp;<em>Poss_{McG}(T_{clone}) = 0</em>, and by Theorem V.1 (ii),&nbsp;<em>Poss_{DM}(T_{clone}) = 0</em>. This recovers the Deutsch–Marletto constructor-theoretic no-cloning theorem from the McGucken framework.&nbsp;<strong>Q.E.D.</strong></p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">VI.3 The Constructor-Theoretic Second Law</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Statement.</strong>&nbsp;Let&nbsp;<em>𝒳_{ordered}</em>&nbsp;be a low-entropy attribute of a thermodynamic system and&nbsp;<em>𝒴_{disordered}</em>&nbsp;be a high-entropy attribute reachable from&nbsp;<em>𝒳_{ordered}</em>&nbsp;by some natural process. <p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>McGucken Derivation.</strong>&nbsp;From Theorem VII.1 of [MG-Thermo], the entropy of an ensemble undergoing Channel-B propagation grows strictly:&nbsp;<em>dS/dt = (3/2)k<sub>B</sub>/t &gt; 0</em>&nbsp;for every&nbsp;<em>t &gt; 0</em>, with the strictness inherited from&nbsp;<em>x<sub>4</sub></em>&#8216;s monotonic advance. <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The reverse task&nbsp;<em>T_-</em>&nbsp;would require a Channel-B propagation chain in which entropy strictly&nbsp;<em>decreases</em>.
